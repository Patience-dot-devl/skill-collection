# Tallyho trial onboarding email sequence

## Summary

The goal of this sequence is activation first and trial-to-paid second. Activation means the user sends a first invoice. Today 38% of trials activate and 11% convert to paid. Taken together, those numbers mean that roughly 29% of activated trials convert (11 divided by 38), assuming most conversions come from activated users. That makes the 62% who never send an invoice the largest opportunity. The sequence therefore splits at the first invoice. Users who have not invoiced receive Track A, which removes setup friction and asks them to send one invoice. Users who have invoiced receive Track B, which helps them get paid and makes the case for continuing at EUR 12 per month.

The sequence runs on the 14-day trial clock. Day 0 is the sign-up date and Day 14 is the day the trial ends. Track A has six emails and Track B has four. Each email has a single primary call to action.

## Assumptions

Because no clarifying questions were possible, I made the following assumptions. Each one should be checked before launch.

1. Day 0 is the sign-up date. The trial ends at the end of Day 14.
2. No card is collected during the trial. A card is requested only when the user chooses to continue after the trial.
3. The copy is in English because the brief was in English. Dutch freelancers may prefer Dutch, so a Dutch version is worth producing before launch.
4. No brand voice guidelines were provided, so the tone is clear, conversational and professional, as the skill's default specifies.
5. The only price given is EUR 12 per month. No annual plan is mentioned, so none is offered.
6. The feature names, the time it takes to send an invoice, the data retention rule and the cancellation terms were not provided. These appear in square brackets and must be confirmed or replaced before sending.
7. The benchmarks in the skill are generic lifecycle figures, not Tallyho data. They are shown only as a reference point.
8. The sequence assumes that someone reads and answers replies to the emails. If nobody does, the reply-based calls to action in A2 and A4 should be removed.
9. Emails are sent at 09:00 in the recipient's local time, unless an event trigger sends them sooner.

**Segment and condition notes:** Send to every new trial. If the first invoice has already been sent by the time this email is due, skip A1 and send B1 instead.

A2b uses the subject line "You are one step away from your first invoice" and a single CTA, "Create my first invoice". ## Branching logic notes

**Entry**
- Sign-up for a trial starts the sequence at A1.

**Track switch**
- The first invoice event exits Track A and starts B1 within one hour.
- If the first invoice happens on Day 11 or later, skip the remaining Track A emails and send B3 and B4 on the trial clock.

**Variants**
- A2b replaces A2 when business details and a client are already complete but no invoice has been sent.
- The "Still getting started?" subject line in A4 is used when the user has opened earlier emails but clicked nothing.
- The customer story variant of A3 is used only if a verified story is approved for use.

**Exits**
- The user pays. Exit the sequence and move the user to the post-conversion welcome flow, which is outside this sequence.
- The user unsubscribes from marketing email. Transactional trial notices may still be sent, depending on your policy.
- A hard bounce removes the user from the sequence.
- The user cancels the trial.

**Suppression**
- Do not send marketing email to a user who has unsubscribed.
- If the user has contacted support in the last 48 hours, hold the next email for 48 hours and then resume it. Do not skip it.
- Do not send a Track A email to a user who has already sent an invoice.

**Re-entry**
- A user who starts a new trial re-enters the sequence at A1.
- A user whose trial ended without payment does not re-enter the onboarding sequence. Send them to a separate win-back sequence, which is not defined here.

## A/B test suggestions

1. **Subject line for A1.** Test the benefit-led subject line ("Welcome to Tallyho: send your first invoice") against the question ("Ready for your first invoice?"). Split new sign-ups 50/50 and measure the share of users who send an invoice within 14 days. Do not use the open rate as the deciding metric. At a baseline of 38% and a target difference of 5 percentage points, you need roughly 1,500 users per arm for 80% power at a 5% significance level.

2. **Help offer in A2 and A4.** Test a reply-based offer against a short call offer. Measure reply rate, time to first invoice and activation. The call offer costs more staff time, so the activation gain has to justify it.

3. **Price framing in A5, A6 and B3.** Test "EUR 12 per month" against "less than EUR 0.40 a day". Measure trial-to-paid conversion. Keep the price figure identical so that only the framing changes.

Run a holdout as well. Keep about 10% of new trials on no onboarding emails. Without a holdout, you cannot tell how much of the 38% activation rate comes from the sequence and how much would have happened anyway.

## Metrics to track

**Primary metric**
- Activation rate: the share of trials that send a first invoice within 14 days. The current figure is 38%.

**Secondary sequence-level metrics**
- Trial-to-paid conversion. The current figure is 11%.
- Conversion among activated users, which is roughly 29% on today's numbers. Confirm this with the actual split.
- Median time from sign-up to first invoice.
- Drop-off by step: sign-up, setup complete, first invoice.
- Share of activated users who send a second invoice.

**Per-email metrics**
- Open rate, click-through rate and unsubscribe rate for each email.
- Reply rate for A2 and A4, if replies are offered.

**Benchmarks**
The skill's generic onboarding benchmarks are an open rate of 50 to 70%, a click-through rate of 10 to 20% and a conversion rate of 15 to 30%. Tallyho's 11% trial-to-paid figure sits below the conversion range, but benchmark definitions vary, so treat these figures as a reference only.

**Review cadence**
Review weekly for the first six weeks after launch, then monthly. Start with activation, because it is the largest gap.
