### Ferrous Postgres Change Data Capture Connector Is Now Generally Available

Anyone who has run Postgres change data capture in production asks two questions. How quickly do changes reach the systems downstream? And what happens to the source database when something goes wrong? Today we can answer both. The Ferrous Postgres change data capture (CDC) connector is generally available. It is open source under the Apache-2.0 licence, and you can also run it as a managed service on Ferrous Cloud.

#### Sub-second Postgres CDC latency, measured end to end

Fresh data is the reason to use CDC. Dashboards, search indexes, caches and event-driven services are only useful when they reflect what is in the database now, not what was there a few minutes ago.

In our benchmark, the Ferrous connector delivered changes with a p95 end-to-end latency of 420 milliseconds at a sustained 20,000 rows per second. "End to end" means the time from a commit in Postgres to the change being available in the destination. In that test, 95 percent of changes arrived in less than half a second, at a throughput that represents a busy production workload.

Benchmarks are a starting point, not a promise. Your results depend on your hardware, network, schema and destination. [Link to the benchmark methodology and the scripts used to reproduce it.] We encourage you to run the connector against your own workload.

#### No more replication-slot bloat

Logical replication in Postgres depends on replication slots. A slot tells Postgres to keep write-ahead log (WAL) until the consumer confirms that it has processed the changes. That guarantee is what makes CDC reliable, and it is also what makes CDC dangerous.

If a slot's confirmed position stops advancing, Postgres keeps every WAL segment generated after that point. This can happen even when the connector is healthy. For example, the tables you capture may be quiet while the rest of the database is busy. WAL grows, disk usage climbs, and eventually someone gets paged, or the primary runs out of space.

The Ferrous connector uses slot heartbeats to stop this. It periodically moves the slot's confirmed position forward, even when the captured tables have no new changes, so Postgres can recycle WAL normally. You get the durability of a replication slot without the slow growth of retained WAL.
