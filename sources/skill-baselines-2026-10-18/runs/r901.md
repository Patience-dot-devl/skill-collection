# Tallyho launch campaign plan

**Product:** Tallyho, invoicing for Dutch freelancers (zzp'ers), EUR 12/month
**Launch:** Tuesday 2027-01-12
**Campaign window:** 6 weeks, 2027-01-12 to 2027-02-22, with a follow-through period to 2027-03-31
**Goal:** 1,500 paid signups by 2027-03-31
**Budget:** EUR 15,000 (all cash spend, including pre-launch work)
**Audience:** Freelancers who invoice from Word or Excel today

## Assumptions

I have made these assumptions. Change them if they are wrong, because several of the numbers below depend on them.

1. A "paid signup" is an account that has paid its first invoice (monthly or annual) on or before 2027-03-31. A trial does not count.
2. Tallyho offers a 14-day free trial. I recommend 14 days instead of 30 because the goal has a fixed end date. With a 30-day trial, nothing started after 1 March can count.
3. EUR 12/month is excluding btw. The product can import a customer list from Excel or CSV and can export invoices for a bookkeeper.
4. The 4,000 waitlist subscribers gave consent for marketing email, and the privacy statement permits uploading hashed emails to ad platforms as a custom audience. If it does not, leave out the lookalike tactics.
5. The team is small: one or two founders and one marketer, with some freelance design and copy help.
6. Today is 2026-10-18. That gives about 12 weeks of preparation before launch, and the plan uses them.

## The short version

- **The waitlist is the campaign.** About half the target, roughly 700 paid signups, has to come from the 4,000 people who already raised their hand. Everything in November and December should keep that list warm, grow it to about 5,000, and turn it into paying "founding members" in the first four weeks.
- **Paid ads are for learning, not for volume.** With no paid-ads history and EUR 15,000 in total, paid media cannot carry a 1,500 target. Realistically it adds 150 to 200 customers. Start small before launch so the accounts have data by January.
- **Partners and referrals fill the gap.** Bookkeepers, freelancer communities and newsletters reach Word and Excel invoicers at a much lower cost than ads. A simple "give a month, get a month" referral programme adds the rest.
- **Use the calendar.** The Q4 btw return is due 2027-01-31, and the income tax season opens on 1 March. Both are natural moments to say "stop doing your invoices in Word".
- **Blended cost target:** EUR 10 per paid signup over the whole campaign (EUR 15,000 / 1,500). Paid channels on their own can run at up to EUR 100 per paid signup and still pay back within about 9 months.

## Is 1,500 realistic?

It is achievable, but only at the top of normal ranges for waitlist conversion. Waitlists for low-priced SaaS usually convert between 5% and 20% to paid. This plan needs about 14% of a 5,000-person list. That is the single biggest risk. Weekly checkpoints, listed further down, show early whether the plan is on track, and they say what to do if it is not.

### Where the 1,500 come from

| Source | Paid signups | Share | What has to be true |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Waitlist (grown to ~5,000 by launch) | 700 | 47% | About 30% of the list starts a trial or buys directly; about 47% of those pay |
| Partners: bookkeepers, communities, newsletters | 250 | 17% | 25 to 40 active bookkeeper partners plus 4 to 6 sponsored newsletters or podcasts |
| Referrals from paying customers | 150 | 10% | About 1 in 7 paying customers brings in one more |
| Organic, PR, founder LinkedIn, direct | 250 | 17% | Launch coverage in Dutch business media and consistent founder posting |
| Paid search and paid social | 150 | 10% | Average cost per paid signup of EUR 50 or less on EUR 7,500 of media |
| **Total** | **1,500** | **100%** | |

Trial-to-paid rates differ a lot by source. Plan for about 45% from the waitlist and partners, about 35% from referrals, and 15% to 25% from cold paid traffic. In total the campaign needs roughly 4,000 to 4,500 trial starts.

### Unit economics

At EUR 12/month and an assumed monthly churn of 4%, a customer stays about 25 months and brings in about EUR 300 in revenue. A cost per paid signup of EUR 100 therefore pays back in about 8 to 9 months. Use EUR 100 as the hard ceiling for any paid channel, and EUR 60 as the target.

## Positioning and offer

### Positioning

Tallyho is not bookkeeping software. It is the fastest way to stop invoicing from Word or Excel. That separates it from full accounting packages such as Moneybird, e-Boekhouden, Jortt, Tellow and SnelStart, which ask a freelancer to learn far more than they need. Several of those competitors have free or cheaper entry tiers, so check their current prices before the launch and do not compete on price. Compete on simplicity and on getting the invoice right.

The core message is: **"Your invoice, correct and sent in two minutes. No more factuur_definitief_v3.docx."**

Three proof points support it:

1. **Always correct.** Every invoice contains what the Belastingdienst requires: a sequential invoice number, KVK number, btw-id, the correct btw rate or the KOR note, and the payment term. In Word, these are exactly the things that go wrong.
2. **Moves your Excel over.** Import your customer list and your current invoice number in one step, so the numbering continues without a gap.
3. **Knows who has not paid.** Automatic payment reminders, and an overview of what is still open before the btw return.

### Example Dutch headlines (for testing)

- "Factureer je nog in Word? Dat kan in 2 minuten." (Do you still invoice in Word? It takes 2 minutes.)
- "Nooit meer een factuurnummer vergeten." (Never forget an invoice number again.)
- "Je btw-aangifte begint bij een goede factuur." (Your btw return starts with a correct invoice.)
- "EUR 12 per maand. Geen boekhoudpakket, gewoon facturen." (EUR 12 a month. Not a bookkeeping package, just invoices.)

### Launch offer

- **Founding member price for the waitlist:** EUR 9/month, fixed for the first 12 months, or EUR 90 for the first year paid annually. The offer is valid until **Tuesday 2027-02-09 at 23:59**. Waitlist members can pay at once without a trial. This gives early paid signups and a clear deadline in week 5.
- **Free switch service** ("Overstapservice") for the first 500 customers: send us your Word or Excel invoice and we rebuild it as a Tallyho template within one working day. This removes the main objection, which is "I like how my invoice looks".
- **Referral:** give one month free, get one month free, with no limit. The cost is revenue that is not collected, not cash, so it does not use the EUR 15,000.
- Do not extend the founding deadline. Extending it damages trust in every later deadline. If conversion is low, add value instead (see the decision rules).

## Preparation: 2026-10-19 to 2027-01-11

### October and November: foundations

- **Tracking.** Set up GA4 or an equivalent, the Meta Conversions API and Google Ads conversion tracking for three events: waitlist signup, trial start and first payment. Make sure the cookie banner and consent mode comply with AVG (GDPR) rules. Add the question "Hoe heb je Tallyho gevonden?" (How did you find Tallyho?) to the signup form. Without ads history, self-reported attribution is the most reliable source.
- **Waitlist health check.** Segment the list by signup date and by whether people opened the most recent email. Send a short "we are almost there" email in early November and remove hard bounces.
- **Waitlist referral.** Give each waitlist subscriber a personal link. Anyone who brings in three new subscribers gets three months free at launch.
- **Landing pages in Dutch.** One main page, one page per audience (Word users, Excel users), and a founding-member page that only the waitlist sees.
- **Lead magnets for Word and Excel users:**
  - A free "factuur-check" (invoice checker): paste or upload your invoice and see which legal requirements are missing. This page doubles as a landing page for paid search.
  - A free Dutch invoice template for Excel and Word with a short line in the footer: "Made with the Tallyho template. Send it in 2 minutes at tallyho.nl." People search for this template in large numbers, so it is a cheap way to reach the exact audience.
- **Partners.** Make a list of 150 bookkeepers and administratiekantoren (small accounting offices) that serve zzp'ers, plus freelancer associations and communities such as ZZP Nederland and PZO, coworking spaces, and freelance platforms. Approach them in November. Aim for 25 to 40 who agree to tell their clients at launch. Give each one a partner link, a short demo and a ready-to-send client email.
- **Sponsorships.** Book 4 to 6 Dutch newsletters or podcasts for freelancers and small entrepreneurs, scheduled for weeks 1 to 4. Choose them on the share of readers who are self-employed, not on total reach.

### 2026-11-16 to 2026-12-13: paid test

- Spend EUR 1,500 to grow the waitlist, not to sell. This gives the ad accounts conversion data and shows which hooks work before the real money goes in.
- Test 3 hooks (Word pain, Excel pain, the btw deadline) by 2 formats (short video, static image) on Meta. On Google, test 10 to 15 keywords.
- Stop the test before mid-December, because advertising costs are high during the holiday period and freelancers are not thinking about invoices.

### 2026-12-14 to 2027-01-11: final preparation

- Recruit 30 to 50 beta users from the waitlist in December to send real invoices. Collect 10 short quotes and 3 video testimonials ("I used Word for 6 years...").
- Prepare the press kit and pitch Dutch business and tech media under embargo for 12 January.
- Load all launch emails, ads and posts, and test every link and tracking event.
- Send the "launch on 12 January" teaser to the waitlist on Tuesday 2027-01-05, not during the holidays.

- Concentrate on trial-to-paid conversion: day 1, 3, 7, 11 and 13 emails, and a personal message to every trial user who has not sent an invoice by day 5.
- Pay out partner commissions and send partners a thank-you update with their numbers.

## Channel plan in detail

### 1. Waitlist email (target: 700 paid)

- Segment into three groups: active (opened in the last 60 days), inactive and new (joined in the paid test). Send the active group first. That protects deliverability and gives early numbers.
- Write as the founder, in plain text or very light HTML, in Dutch. Each email has one call to action.
- Sequence: teaser on 01-05, launch on 01-12, reminder on 01-14, "how it works" video on 01-19, btw deadline on 01-26, proof on 02-02, countdown on 02-07, last call on 02-09 (morning and evening), and switch service on 02-16.
- Trigger: if fewer than 250 waitlist members have paid by 2027-01-25, see the decision rules.

### 2. Partners (target: 250 paid)

- **Bookkeepers** are the strongest partner. They receive messy Word and Excel invoices every quarter, so a client using Tallyho saves them time. Give them a co-branded client email, a 3-minute demo video and EUR 25 per paid client.
- **Communities and associations:** offer an exclusive member discount (for example, the first month free) in exchange for a newsletter mention or a webinar.
- **Sponsored newsletters and podcasts:** use a unique code and link per placement so the result of each one is clear. Do not rebook a placement that costs more than EUR 100 per paid signup.

### 3. Referrals (target: 150 paid)

- Ask for a referral right after a customer's first invoice is paid, because that is the moment of greatest satisfaction. Do not ask on day 1.
- Show the referral link in the app, in the payment confirmation email and in the footer of every invoice sent with Tallyho. The footer line is optional for customers and can be turned off. Every invoice then reaches another business, and many of those clients are freelancers too.

### 4. Organic, PR and founder content (target: 250 paid)

- PR: one launch story for Dutch business and tech media, built around a data point. For example, run a survey among the waitlist on how many freelancers still invoice from Word or Excel and how many invoices are paid late.
- The founder posts on LinkedIn three times a week: build-in-public, customer stories and short how-to videos.
- SEO is a long-term channel and will not deliver much in six weeks. The invoice checker and the template pages are still worth building now, because they also improve the quality of paid search landing pages.

### 5. Paid search and paid social (target: 150 paid, EUR 8,000 including the test)

**Google Search (EUR 4,000 after launch)**
- Keywords with high intent: "factuur maken zzp", "facturatieprogramma zzp", "factuur programma", "factuur maken online", "facturen versturen zzp".
- Problem keywords leading to the lead magnets: "factuur template excel", "factuur maken in word", "factuur eisen belastingdienst". These convert to email, not straight to trial, so measure them on cost per email address.
- Bid on the brand name "Tallyho" to protect it.
- Do not bid on competitor brand names during the launch. It is expensive, and those searchers are often existing customers of the competitor.
- Planning assumptions, to replace with real data after week 2: cost per click EUR 1.50 to 3.00, 8% to 12% of clicks start a trial, and 25% to 30% of trials pay. That gives roughly EUR 40 to EUR 150 per paid signup.

**Meta (EUR 2,500 after launch)**
- Audiences: lookalike of the waitlist (1% Netherlands), interest-based freelancer audiences aged 25 to 55, and retargeting of site visitors and trial users who did not pay.
- Creative: short vertical videos showing a Word invoice turning into a Tallyho invoice in two minutes, filmed with real freelancers if possible.
- Optimise on trial start, not on payment. A new account cannot reach enough payment events per week to leave the learning phase.
- Planning assumptions: cost per click EUR 0.60 to 1.20, 3% to 5% of clicks start a trial, and 15% to 25% of trials pay. That gives roughly EUR 50 to EUR 250 per paid signup. Meta will probably be more expensive than search, so most of its value is in retargeting.

**LinkedIn Ads:** not recommended. The cost per click in the Netherlands is usually several times that of Meta, and the budget is too small. Use LinkedIn organically through the founder.

## Measurement and checkpoints

### Weekly dashboard (every Monday)

- Cumulative paid signups compared with target
- Trial starts by source, using UTM data plus the "how did you find us" answer
- Trial-to-paid rate by source
- Cost per trial and cost per paid signup by paid channel
- Waitlist: open rate, click rate and the share that has paid
- Activation: the share of trials that send a first invoice within 3 days. This is the best early predictor of payment.

### Decision rules

- **Waitlist below plan:** if fewer than 250 waitlist members have paid by 2027-01-25, the founder emails the 500 most engaged non-buyers personally, the team runs two live "switch with us" sessions, and the free switch service is brought forward to the waitlist. Do not cut the price further or extend the deadline.
- **A paid channel above EUR 100 per paid signup** after EUR 1,000 of spend: cut it back to brand terms and retargeting, and move the money to the reserve.
- **A paid channel under EUR 60 per paid signup:** raise its daily budget by no more than 20% to 30% at a time, so the learning phase does not restart.
- **Trial-to-paid below 30% overall in week 2:** the problem is the product experience, not marketing. Look at where trial users stop, and contact every trial user who has not sent an invoice by day 5.
- **On 2027-02-22, cumulative paid below 850:** the goal will probably not be met. Tell stakeholders then, not in late March. Then put the rest of the budget into the partner and referral channels, which have the best remaining return, and set a realistic end figure.

## Main risks

| Risk | Effect | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| The waitlist is old or cold | Largest source falls short | Warm-up emails from November, segmentation, waitlist referral, a growth test that brings in new subscribers |
| No paid-ads history | High costs in the first weeks | Pre-launch test, small daily budgets at launch, optimise on trial start, strict cost ceiling |
| Competitors with free or cheaper tiers | Price objections | Position as "not a bookkeeping package"; switch service; legally correct invoices as the reason to pay |
| Trial users do not activate | Trials do not turn into payments | Excel import, switch service, onboarding emails, personal follow-up on day 5 |
| The team cannot do everything | Actions slip | Founder owns partners and PR; marketer owns email and ads; freelance help for creative. Everything in weeks 1 and 2 is prepared before 2027-01-11 |
| Email deliverability problems at launch | Launch emails go to spam | Warm up the sending domain in November and December, set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and send to the active segment first |
