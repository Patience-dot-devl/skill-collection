# Competitive Brief: Linear's Free Tier and What It Means for Trackwise

**Research date:** 2026-10-18
**Competitor:** Linear (linear.app)
**Our product:** Trackwise, an issue tracker for small development agencies (5 to 30 people) at USD 8 per seat, with built-in client portals and time tracking.
**Question:** What does Linear's new free tier for teams of up to 10 people mean for us, and how should we respond in the next 30 days?

## Assumptions and data caveats

- **I took the launch as given, as you asked.** One thing to check: on 2026-10-18, Linear's live pricing page shows a different Free plan. It allows **unlimited members**, 2 teams and **250 issues**, and adds Linear's agent platform. The paid plans are Basic at USD 10 per user per month and Business at USD 16 per user per month, both billed yearly. One third-party pricing site (automationatlas.io) mentions a 10-user cap. Before we put any claim about Linear's terms into public copy, someone must read the announcement and record the exact limits: the issue cap, the number of teams, guests and integrations.
- I assume the new free tier leaves out **guest accounts**. Today, Linear lists "private teams and guests" under the Business plan (USD 16). If that holds, an agency on Linear's free tier cannot give clients access without moving to Business.
- I assume Trackwise's USD 8 per seat is billed monthly, that client users in our portal do not need a paid seat, and that we have no free plan today.
- I had no access to our CRM, win/loss data or product analytics. The figures below are list-price comparisons, not observed customer behaviour.

## 1. Executive summary

Linear's free tier takes away our price advantage for the smallest third of our target market: agencies with 5 to 10 people. A developer can now say "Linear is free, why are we paying for Trackwise?", and the question has an easy answer only if we have prepared one. Linear is still a product-development tool built for in-house product teams. It does not offer time tracking or a client-facing portal, and its homepage never mentions agencies or clients. **The biggest threat** is that small agencies sign up for Linear free at the top of the funnel and never evaluate us, and that our 5 to 10 seat customers ask hard questions at renewal. **The biggest opportunity** is to stop competing as "a cheaper issue tracker" and position Trackwise as the system that runs an agency's client work, a category Linear has not claimed.

## 2. Competitor profile: Linear

### Company overview
- **Positioning:** "The product development system for teams and agents." The subheadline is "Purpose-built for planning and building products. Designed for the AI era."
- **Target audience:** In-house software product teams, from startups up to companies such as OpenAI and Ramp, which it names on the homepage. It claims "over 40,000 product teams."
- **Stage:** A well-funded, category-leading brand with very strong word of mouth among developers.
- **Key recent developments:** A strong push into AI. The homepage features agents as teammates, Linear Agent, coding sessions, Linear MCP and code review. It also offers more for larger customers, including Insights, Asks, Triage Intelligence and SAML/SCIM on Enterprise. The new free tier for up to 10 people is the latest move.

### Messaging analysis
- **Headline:** "The product development system for teams and agents."
- **Core value proposition:** A fast, focused, opinionated tool that helps product teams plan and ship, now with AI agents doing part of the work.
- **Key themes:** purpose-built for product teams; agents as teammates; speed and focus; proof from well-known customers.
- **Tone and voice:** Minimal, confident, design-led, close to premium. Linear writes for engineers and product people who care about craft.
- **Problem it describes:** Noise, slowness and bloated legacy tools (in effect, Jira) that keep product teams from shipping.
- **Narrative:** The villain is slow, bloated tooling. The hero is the high-performing product team. The change it promises is going from noise to focused shipping, and now from manual work to work delegated to agents.

### Product and pricing positioning
- **Category:** Product development system, not "issue tracker" or "project management".
- **Features it emphasises:** intake and triage, roadmaps and initiatives, cycles, AI agents, Git and pull-request automation, and Customer Requests (which means *end-user feedback*, not agency clients).
- **Pricing approach:** A free plan to land teams, then per-seat plans billed yearly (Basic USD 10, Business USD 16). Guests and private teams sit behind Business. That matters for agencies.

### Content strategy (from general knowledge; I did not audit it in depth today)
- A frequent public changelog, essays about how to build products (the "Linear Method"), and a well-known design and brand presence on X and LinkedIn.
- The themes are craft, focus, how good teams work, and now AI agents in product development.
- On search, it targets "issue tracking", "Jira alternative", "product roadmap" and "sprint planning" terms. It has no content aimed at agencies, client delivery or billable work.

### Strengths
- Developers prefer it, and that preference often decides the tool at a small agency.
- An excellent user experience, speed, and integrations with GitHub and Slack, which reviewers on G2 and TrustRadius praise again and again.
- A strong brand, and an AI story that we cannot match on budget.
- Now a USD 0 entry price for small teams.

### Weaknesses
- **Built for product teams, not agencies.** Reviewers say it is optimised for software and product teams and that non-technical users find it awkward. Agency account managers and clients are non-technical users.
- **No time tracking.** A G2 summary names time tracking as a missing capability.
- **No client portal.** Client access means guest accounts, which are on Business at USD 16 per seat.
- Reviewers also criticise its reporting, dashboards and customisation, and some worry that it is drifting toward enterprise features and bloat.

## 3. What the free tier does to the economics

List-price monthly cost for the internal team only. I assume that once a team passes 10 people, every Linear seat becomes paid. Linear prices are yearly-billed rates.

| Agency size | Trackwise (USD 8) | Linear free / Basic (USD 10) | Linear Business (USD 16, includes guests) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 people | USD 40 | USD 0 | USD 80 |
| 10 people | USD 80 | USD 0 | USD 160 |
| 11 people | USD 88 | USD 110 | USD 176 |
| 20 people | USD 160 | USD 200 | USD 320 |
| 30 people | USD 240 | USD 300 | USD 480 |

What this shows:

1. **At 5 to 10 people we lose on price alone**, unless the buyer values time tracking and client access. On Linear, an agency that wants clients inside the tool has to pay for Business (USD 80 to 160 a month at this size).
2. **At 11 people and above we are cheaper than Linear on every plan.** Linear's free tier also creates an upgrade cliff: an agency that hires its eleventh person goes from USD 0 to at least USD 110 a month. That is a moment we can target.
3. **The real comparison is a stack.** An agency on Linear free still needs a time tracker and some way to share progress with clients, such as a separate portal, spreadsheets or status emails. Each of those costs money or staff time. Our price should be compared with that whole stack, not with Linear alone.

|---|---|---|
| Primary tagline | (current; to sharpen, see below) | "The product development system for teams and agents" |
| Target buyer | Owner or operations lead at a 5 to 30 person dev agency | Engineering and product leaders at in-house product teams |
| Key differentiator | Client portals and time tracking built into the issue tracker | Speed, craft, AI agents |
| Tone and voice | Practical, agency-operational (assumed) | Minimal, premium, engineering-led |
| Core value proposition | One tool from client request to billable hour | Plan and ship products faster with humans and agents |
| Price entry point | USD 8 per seat | USD 0 for up to 10 people (as given) |

**Positioning statements**

- Linear (reverse-engineered): *For software product teams, Linear is the product development system that helps teams plan and ship with focus, because it is purpose-built, fast and designed for working with AI agents.*
- Trackwise (proposed): *For development agencies that build software for clients, Trackwise is the delivery workspace that connects client requests, development work and billable time in one place, because client portals and time tracking are built into the tracker instead of bolted on.*

**Positioning map (product team focus vs agency/client focus, and tracker only vs full delivery workflow):** Linear sits at "product team, tracker plus AI". Trackwise should own "agency, full client-delivery workflow". Nobody in Linear's tier of brand strength occupies that corner. That is the niche to defend. We should not try to win the general issue-tracker category.

## 6. Opportunities

- **Linear does not speak to agencies at all.** Its site never says "agency" or "client". Search terms such as "Linear for agencies", "Linear client portal", "Linear time tracking" and "Linear alternative for agencies" are unclaimed, and the launch will send more people to search for them.
- **The client is our wedge.** Agencies win and keep work by keeping clients informed. Linear charges USD 16 per seat for guest access. Our portal is built in.
- **The upgrade cliff at 11 people.** Agencies that grow past 10 people face a sudden bill. We can be the obvious, cheaper and better-fitting choice at that moment.
- **Coexistence instead of replacement.** Some agencies will let developers use Linear free whatever we do. A Linear sync, or even a CSV import, would let Trackwise be the client and time layer on top. That turns a threat into a funnel. It is a strategic option to scope, not something to ship within 30 days.

## 7. Threats

- **Price anchoring.** "Free" resets what small teams expect to pay for issue tracking and makes USD 8 per seat feel like a cost instead of a bargain.
- **Fewer leads at the top of the funnel.** Small agencies, especially new ones, will start on Linear by default, and we will not see them until they hurt.
- **Renewal risk in our 5 to 10 seat accounts.** These customers save the most by leaving, and developers inside them may push to switch.
- **Developer preference and AI momentum.** Linear's AI features make it look like the modern choice, and we cannot outspend it on that story.
- **Possible follow-up moves.** If Linear later adds guests to the free tier, or ships time tracking or a client view, our wedge narrows. We should watch for this every month.

## 8. Recommended response: the next 30 days

**Main recommendation:** Do not drop to free as a reflex. Hold the price, make "built for agencies" explicit everywhere, protect our smallest accounts, and test a low-risk entry offer. A price war against a better-funded brand would lower our revenue and leave our positioning just as unclear.

### Week 1: Confirm and contain (quick wins)
1. **Verify the facts.** Record the exact limits of Linear's free tier (issues, teams, guests, integrations) and the date in an internal note. Fix our claims to that record.
2. **Size our exposure.** Pull every account with 10 or fewer seats. Note renewal dates, portal use and time-tracking use. Accounts that do not use the portal or time tracking are at highest risk, because they are using us as a plain tracker.
3. **Brief sales and support.** Share a one-page battlecard (draft below) and an agreed answer to "Linear is free now".
4. **Publish a clear, factual response page:** "Linear vs Trackwise for agencies". Name what Linear does well. Show the cost table and the client and time workflow. Do not attack Linear.

### Week 2: Protect the base
5. **Contact at-risk small accounts.** Customer success should call or email the 5 to 10 seat accounts with low feature use. Offer a 30-minute session to set up the client portal and time tracking. Customers who use those features have a reason to stay that Linear cannot match.
6. **Use a targeted retention offer, not a public price cut.** For example, offer accounts with 10 or fewer seats that renew annually in the next 90 days a fixed price for 12 months or one free month. Track how many take it.

### Weeks 3 and 4: Reposition and test
7. **Update the homepage and ads to the agency message**, for example: "The issue tracker that runs your client work: portals and billable time built in." Test it against the current headline.
8. **Launch content for the search gap:** a "Linear for agencies" comparison page, a guide to giving clients visibility without paying per guest seat, and a cost calculator that compares Linear plus a time tracker plus a portal tool with Trackwise.
9. **Test an entry offer before deciding on a free plan.** Options to compare: a free plan for up to 3 internal users with one client portal; a longer trial (30 days instead of the standard); or a "first 5 seats at half price for 6 months" offer for new agencies. Run one as a time-boxed experiment and measure trial-to-paid conversion and the size of the accounts it attracts.
10. **Scope the coexistence option.** Talk to 5 to 10 customers or prospects whose developers use Linear. Find out whether a Linear sync, with Trackwise providing the portal and time tracking, would win or keep them. Decide by day 30 whether it goes on the roadmap.

### What to measure over the 30 days
- Monthly churn and downgrades among accounts with 10 or fewer seats, compared with the previous quarter.
- Trial sign-ups and trial-to-paid conversion, split by agency size.
- How often "Linear" comes up in lost deals and cancellation reasons. Add it as a tagged reason now.
- Traffic and conversions on the comparison page and calculator.
- Portal and time-tracking adoption in small accounts after the outreach.

## Draft battlecard: Trackwise vs Linear (small agencies)

**Last updated:** 2026-10-18

**Their pitch:** "The product development system for teams and agents." Fast, beautiful, AI-forward, and now free for small teams.

**Where they are genuinely strong:** developer experience, speed, GitHub and Slack integrations, AI agents, brand.

**Where they fall short for agencies:** no time tracking; no client portal (client access needs guest seats on Business at USD 16 per seat); built for in-house product teams, so account managers and clients find it awkward; the free tier ends at 10 people.

**Our differentiators**
1. **Client portal built in.** Clients see progress and submit requests without paid seats. This matters because it cuts status meetings and protects the relationship. To prove it, show a demo portal and a customer quote.
2. **Time tracking on the issue.** Hours are logged where the work happens. This matters because it makes invoices accurate and utilisation visible. To prove it, show a time report from a real (anonymised) account.
3. **Priced for growing agencies.** USD 8 per seat with no cliff at 11 people. This matters because costs stay predictable as the agency hires. The cost table is the proof.

**Objection handling**

| If the prospect says... | Respond with... |
|---|---|
| "Linear is free now." | "For up to 10 people, yes. How do your clients see progress today, and where do you track billable hours? With Linear you would add a time tracker and pay for guest seats or a separate portal. Trackwise includes both, and it costs less than Linear once you pass 10 people." |
| "Our developers love Linear." | "They have good reason: it is a great tracker. The question is whether your account managers and clients can work in it too. Would you like to see what your client would see in Trackwise?" |
| "Linear has AI agents." | "Linear is investing heavily in AI for product teams. For an agency, the costly problems are scope creep, unclear client approvals and unbilled hours. That is where Trackwise focuses." |

**Questions to ask early (landmines to set)**
- "How do clients submit requests and approve work today?"
- "How confident are you that every billable hour ends up on an invoice?"
- "What happens to your tooling costs when you hire your eleventh person?"

**Questions to prepare for (landmines to defuse)**
- "Why should we pay when Linear is free?" Answer with the cost of the full stack and the time saved on client updates, not with a discount.
- "Is Trackwise as fast and polished as Linear?" Be honest. Show the core workflow live, and point to the client and time features Linear does not have.
