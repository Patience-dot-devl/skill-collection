# Tallyho Launch Campaign Brief (6 weeks from 2027-01-12)

## Assumptions

Before the brief itself, these are the assumptions I made because the request did not settle them. Each one should be confirmed before budget is committed.

1. "1,500 paid signups by 2027-03-31" means 1,500 paying subscribers who are active on 2027-03-31, counted from launch onward. If the target counts gross sign-ups instead of active paying accounts, the plan is easier, but the number is less meaningful. If it is net of churn, the plan is harder, and I have flagged where that matters.
2. The EUR 12 price is excluding 21% BTW, because the buyers are businesses (zzp'ers) who can usually reclaim input VAT. If the price is inclusive, the revenue per subscriber is lower.
3. The EUR 15,000 budget covers all marketing spend in the period from launch to 2027-03-31. It does not include staff time, the product itself, or the cost of the waitlist tool.
4. The 4,000-person waitlist contains people who opted in to receive launch and product updates. I have not verified the consent wording. This matters under the GDPR (AVG) and the Dutch cookie and e-mail rules, and it must be checked before any e-mail goes out.
5. The campaign is in Dutch. All copy, ads and landing pages are written for Dutch freelancers. The brand name "Tallyho" has not been checked for trademark conflicts, and that check should happen before the name is printed on paid media.
6. There is no paid-ads history, so every paid benchmark below comes from outside sources and is only directional.
7. The campaign calendar uses Monday-to-Sunday weeks. Week 1 starts on Monday 2027-01-11, the Monday before the launch date. The launch date, Tuesday 2027-01-12, falls inside Week 1.

## Reality check on the target

The goal is 1,500 paying subscribers, which equals EUR 18,000 in monthly recurring revenue (MRR) at EUR 12 per month. That is an ambitious result for a first launch with a EUR 15,000 budget, and the arithmetic shows why.

- Paid media alone cannot carry the goal. A Superads benchmark puts the average cost per lead for SaaS and cloud platforms in the Netherlands at about EUR 33 over October 2024 to October 2025, although the source notes that the series is volatile and modelled ([Superads, NL SaaS CPL](https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cost-per-lead/saas-cloud-platforms/netherlands)). A cross-market TrendTrack benchmark lists Technology and SaaS at a cost per acquisition of USD 55.21 ([TrendTrack, Meta benchmark 2025](https://www.trendtrack.io/nl/blog-post/meta-advertentiebudget-per-branche-2025-het-ultieme-benchmarkrapport)). If EUR 6,000 of paid media buys about 180 leads at EUR 33 each, and roughly 20% of those leads pay, that is about 35 paying subscribers. Paid media is therefore a test and a support channel, not the engine.
- The waitlist is the main lever. With 4,000 people, a launch-window conversion of 20% gives 800 paying subscribers. Conversion of 12% gives 480 and conversion of 30% gives 1,200.
- The remainder must come from partnerships with accountants and bookkeepers, referrals, owned content, search, and the tail of the period after the campaign.

Scenario forecast for paying subscribers active on 2027-03-31:

| Scenario | Waitlist conversion | Other sources (paid, partners, organic, referral) | Total | MRR at EUR 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 480 (12%) | 350 | 830 | EUR 9,960 |
| Base | 800 (20%) | 500 | 1,300 | EUR 15,600 |
| High | 1,200 (30%) | 700 | 1,900 | EUR 22,800 |

The target of 1,500 sits between the base and high cases. I recommend treating 1,500 as a stretch target and setting a decision point at the end of Week 4 (2027-02-07). If the waitlist conversion is below 15% by then, the plan should shift budget toward partnerships and search and the target should be re-forecast with the business owner.

Churn also matters. If 1,500 means active subscribers and monthly churn is 4%, the team needs to add about 60 to 70 net new subscribers per month just to stay level, on top of the growth needed to reach the target. Churn should be tracked from the first week.

## 1. Campaign Overview

**Campaign name suggestion:** "Factuur in een klik" ("Invoice in one click"). The name works in Dutch and matches the core promise. An alternative is "Klaar met Word" ("Done with Word"), which speaks directly to the audience's current pain.

**One-sentence summary:** In the first six weeks after launch, Tallyho converts its waitlist and reaches Dutch freelancers who still build invoices in Word or Excel, using early-access offers, partnerships, search, and a deliberately limited paid test.

**Primary objective:** 1,500 paying subscribers active on 2027-03-31, with at least 800 of them coming from the waitlist by 2027-02-21 (end of Week 6). Price: EUR 12 per month.

**Secondary objectives:**
- Reach a waitlist-to-paid conversion of at least 20% within the six-week campaign window.
- Get at least 60% of new accounts to send their first invoice within 48 hours of sign-up.
- Keep blended acquisition cost (total spend divided by new paying subscribers) at or below EUR 10 across the period. This is EUR 15,000 divided by 1,500.
- Build a partner channel of at least 20 accountants or bookkeepers who refer clients.

## 2. Target Audience

**Primary segment:** Solo self-employed Dutch professionals (zzp'ers) who issue between 10 and 80 invoices a year and currently build them in Word or Excel. They work as consultants, designers, developers, coaches, therapists, interim managers and tradespeople. They are comfortable with software but are not accountants. They already know that invoices must carry certain legal details, and they worry about making a mistake that the Belastingdienst would notice.

The market is large but shrinking slightly. CBS puts the number of zzp'ers at about 1.2 million in 2025, down about 62,000 from the year before, with the steepest drop among young self-employed people ([CBS, Dossier ZZP](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/dossier/dossier-zzp); [zzp-nederland.nl](https://www.zzp-nederland.nl/nieuws/aantal-zzpers-daalt-2025-vooral-onder-jongeren)). The total addressable base is therefore a few hundred thousand businesses that invoice manually, and the waitlist is a meaningful slice of it.

**Secondary segment:** Small bookkeepers and accountants who look after several zzp'ers and might recommend or resell Tallyho. This segment brings referral volume rather than direct sign-ups.

**Pain points and motivations:**
- Invoices are slow to make and easy to get wrong. Numbering gets messed up, and they forget to add a field.
- Payment reminders are manual and awkward, so cash arrives late.
- Their accountant asks for clean records at year end and at VAT time.
- They want something cheap and simple, without a bloated accounting suite.
- A EUR 12 price point is easy to justify, but only if the time saved is obvious.

**Where they spend time:** LinkedIn, Facebook groups for zzp'ers and freelancers, Instagram for creative freelancers, Google search when they have a specific problem, Dutch business newsletters, and conversations with their accountant.

**Buying stage:** Most of the waitlist is in the consideration stage, because they already asked for early access. Most of the cold audience is in awareness. The campaign therefore leans on decision-stage messaging for the waitlist and on problem-aware messaging for everyone else.

## 3. Key Messages

**Core message (one sentence):** Tallyho lets you send a professional, legally complete invoice in under two minutes, for EUR 12 a month, without the spreadsheet.

**Supporting messages:**
1. **Time back.** Making an invoice in Word takes longer than it should, and copying last month's file is where mistakes start. Tallyho stores your clients and your items, so each new invoice takes a few clicks.
2. **Fewer mistakes.** Automatic numbering and required fields help keep invoices consistent. Proof point needed: a description of how numbering works, which the product team must confirm.
3. **Get paid faster.** Built-in payment reminders and a payment link shorten the time to cash. Proof point needed: the product must actually support this at launch. Do not claim it otherwise.
4. **Priced for one person.** EUR 12 a month with no per-invoice fees and no bundled accounting suite. This is a clear contrast with larger tools that charge more and do more than a freelancer needs.

**Message variations by channel:**
- Waitlist e-mail: personal and founder-led. "You asked for early access. It is ready." Lead with the early-access offer and a clear deadline.
- Meta ads: visual and direct. Show the Word document and the Tallyho invoice side by side, and use a short problem statement.
- Google search: functional. Answer the query directly and match the phrase the searcher used.
- Partner materials: professional and practical. Emphasise cleaner client records and less chasing for the accountant's clients.
- LinkedIn: founder voice, with specifics on what was built and why.

**Proof points:** The brief contains no verified customer numbers, testimonials or awards, because none were supplied. Every claim in ads and landing pages must be backed by a real source before publication. Until then, use placeholders such as "[TO CONFIRM: beta user count]" and do not publish them. Legal review of any statement about invoice compliance is required. Invoice content under Dutch VAT rules must be described accurately, and the product should not claim that it guarantees compliance.

**Timing hook:** The Q4 2026 BTW (VAT) return is due on 31 January 2027, which is a Sunday. This falls in Week 3 of the campaign. A Belastingdienst date check is still required before it appears in any copy, but it is a natural reason to act now ([zzp-nederland.nl, 31 januari deadline](https://www.zzp-nederland.nl/nieuws/31-januari-deadline-btw-aangifte-en-betaling-waar-moet-je-op-letten)).

## 4. Channel Strategy

**Owned**

- **Waitlist e-mail (4,000 contacts).** This is the highest-value channel by a wide margin. Effort: low to medium. Why it fits: these people already asked for the product. Format: three to five e-mails across the launch window, plus a short onboarding series. Budget: email platform and copy only, within the tools and production lines below.
- **Website and landing page.** Effort: medium. Why it fits: every other channel sends traffic here. Format: a clear page with a sample invoice, a price block, an early-access offer, and a short FAQ. Include Dutch invoice requirements in plain language to earn trust.
- **Blog and search content.** Effort: medium. Why it fits: searches such as "factuur maken zzp" and "factuursjabloon Word zzp" show people looking for exactly this switch. Format: three to four articles. These are slower to pay off but keep working after the window closes.
- **LinkedIn (founder and company pages).** Effort: medium. Format: three posts a week, including one build-in-public update per week.

**Earned**

- **Accountant and bookkeeper partnerships.** Effort: medium to high. Why it fits: accountants see the pain every quarter and can recommend a tool to clients. Format: a referral offer with a discount for their clients and a commission or free-months arrangement for the partner. Target: 20 partners by Week 4.
- **zzp communities and newsletters.** Effort: medium. Format: sponsored or organic posts in two or three established Dutch freelancer newsletters and Facebook groups where moderators permit it. Budget: part of the partnerships line.
- **Press and product launch platforms.** Effort: medium. Format: a Dutch tech and business press pitch in Week 1, and a launch listing on a product directory such as Product Hunt on the launch day. Keep the press pitch focused on the zzp angle, not the software.
- **Referral programme.** Effort: low. Format: a free month for both referrer and referred. Launch in Week 2, after the first wave of customers has an experience to share.

**Paid**

- **Meta (Facebook and Instagram).** Effort: medium. Why it fits: good reach in the zzp audience and the ability to build a lookalike audience from the waitlist and early customers. Format: video or carousel showing invoice creation in under two minutes, plus a retargeting ad for landing page visitors. Budget: about 60% of the paid allocation.
- **Google Search.** Effort: medium. Why it fits: it captures people who already want to replace their current process. Format: a small set of Dutch keyword campaigns on high-intent phrases, with exact and phrase match only at first. Budget: about 40% of the paid allocation.
- **Testing reserve.** About EUR 1,000 is held back for one new audience or placement test, decided at the Week 2 review.

Paid channels should run as tests with a stop-loss rule. If the blended cost per paying subscriber from paid channels is above EUR 60 after two weeks of data, shift the budget toward search and partners.

**Channel priority, from highest to lowest expected return per euro:** waitlist e-mail, partnerships, search, referrals, LinkedIn organic, Meta retargeting, Meta cold prospecting, press.

## 7. Success Metrics

**Primary KPI:** 1,500 paying subscribers active on 2027-03-31 (about EUR 18,000 MRR).

**Secondary KPIs:**
1. **Waitlist-to-paid conversion.** Target 20% by 2027-02-21. Track weekly.
2. **Activation rate.** Share of new accounts that send a first invoice within 48 hours. Target 60%.
3. **Blended acquisition cost.** Total marketing spend divided by new paying subscribers. Target at or below EUR 10 over the full period, and below EUR 60 for paid channels alone.
4. **Partner-sourced subscribers.** Target 150 by 2027-03-31 from at least 20 partners.
5. **Monthly churn.** Target below 5% in the first three months. Track from the first billing cycle.

**Tracking:**
- UTM parameters on every link, with a naming convention agreed before launch.
- Server-side conversion events for paid subscriptions, sent to Meta through the Conversions API and to Google through its conversion tag.
- Consent-compliant analytics, using a consent mode setup for Dutch and EU visitors.
- A simple attribution rule: last non-direct click for paid, referral code for partners, and a self-reported "how did you hear about us" question at sign-up.

**Reporting cadence:** Daily dashboard checks in Week 1, weekly review every Monday for the rest of the campaign, a formal decision point at the end of Week 4, and monthly reporting after the campaign ends.

Historical benchmarks: no product analytics or past campaign data was supplied, so the targets above are planning assumptions and should be replaced with real data once the soft launch in December produces it.

## 9. Risks and Mitigations

1. **Waitlist conversion falls below 15%.** This is the biggest risk because the plan depends on it. Mitigation: confirm interest with a short survey before launch, use a time-limited early-access price, send a founder-led personal e-mail, and offer a short call for people who have questions. If conversion is still low by Week 4, move budget into partnerships and search and re-forecast the target.

2. **Paid acquisition is too expensive for the target.** The benchmarks suggest cost per lead near EUR 33 and cost per acquisition well above that. Mitigation: start with search and retargeting, which convert better, cap the cold prospecting test, apply the EUR 60 stop-loss rule, and keep the testing reserve separate so it can be stopped at any time.

3. **Launch bugs or weak onboarding slow activation.** A broken checkout or a confusing first invoice wastes the launch traffic. Mitigation: run the soft launch in December with about 200 people, fix the top issues, add a guided first invoice, and keep a support channel staffed for the first two weeks. Do not start paid media until the checkout has passed end-to-end testing.

Additional risk to note: freelancers may see free Word and Excel templates as good enough, which weakens the case for EUR 12 a month. The messaging should focus on time saved, consistent numbering, and getting paid faster, rather than on features alone.
