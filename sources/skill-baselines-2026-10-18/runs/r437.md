# Tallyho launch campaign: 6-week plan to 1,500 paid signups by 2027-03-31

## 1. Summary

The plan below uses the 4,000-person waitlist as the main engine, supports it with referrals, partners, creators, search and social ads, and builds organic search content for the long term. The honest conclusion is that the goal of 1,500 paid signups is not reachable with EUR 15,000 of paid media alone. At EUR 15,000 across 1,500 signups, the budget would need to cost EUR 10 per paid signup. Paid acquisition for a EUR 12 per month product with no ad history usually costs far more than that. The base case in this plan lands at roughly 1,100 to 1,200 paid signups by 2027-03-31, with an upside case near 1,450. Section 7 sets out the options for closing the gap.

## 2. Assumptions

The request left several things open, so I made the following assumptions. Please check them before using the numbers.

1. The EUR 15,000 covers all paid media, creator fees, partner commissions, and campaign tooling from 2027-01-12 to 2027-03-31. It excludes launch discounts, referral credits, and internal staff time.
2. The 6-week launch sprint runs from Tuesday 2027-01-12 to Monday 2027-02-22. The sustain phase runs from 2027-02-23 to 2027-03-31.
3. A "paid signup" means a customer whose first subscription payment has succeeded by 2027-03-31, net of refunds. Free trial starts are tracked separately.
4. The 4,000 waitlist members opted in within roughly the last 12 months and still want the product. The waitlist is not a list of cold contacts.
5. The price of EUR 12 per month is excluding BTW for business customers. The launch offer discussed in section 4 is a recommendation, not a decided fact.
6. I found no Netherlands-specific benchmark for SaaS ad costs. The cost figures below are planning assumptions, to be replaced with real numbers after the first two weeks of spend.
7. Tallyho has no paid-ads history, so the Meta pixel, conversion API, and attribution must be set up before launch.

## 3. Market context

Two facts shape the plan. First, the pool of freelancers is shrinking. News reports citing CBS say that about 1.5 million people held a main job as self-employed in Q2 2026, which is 131,000 fewer than at the end of 2024. Sources disagree on the full-year 2025 figure (71,000 or 100,000 fewer), so I would not quote a precise number in public. The implication is that growth has to come from people who already invoice, mostly from Word and Excel, and not from market growth.

Second, the competitive price points are moving. Moneybird removed its free starter plan on 2026-07-01, and its paid plans start at about EUR 3 per month on an annual plan, with transaction limits that push heavier users higher. Rompslomp's paid Puur Factuur plan rises from EUR 7.95 to EUR 8.95 from 2026-11-01, and its free starter plan adds a EUR 1.50 charge per invoice from the same date. Jortt's paid plans sit roughly between EUR 17.50 and EUR 34.95 according to a Finom comparison, which may be out of date. These figures came from search results that disagree with each other in places, so any price comparison in an ad must be checked against each vendor's live pricing page before it is published. The useful message is that Tallyho is simple and fixed-price, which matters more to a Word or Excel user than a low headline price.

## 4. Offer and positioning

The core message for the Word and Excel audience is that invoicing should take a minute, not an evening of copying figures into a template. Tallyho should promise fewer steps and fewer errors, without claiming tax advice it cannot give. Any figure such as "in 60 seconds" needs to be measured on the real product and kept in the claim file.

The launch offer I recommend is the first three months at EUR 9 per month for anyone who joins during the sprint, with the offer closing on 2027-02-28. This is a price cost of EUR 9 per customer over three months, and it should be counted as a revenue discount, outside the marketing budget. A referral mechanic runs alongside it: a paying customer gets one free month for each referred customer who pays, and the referred customer gets one free month too. The cost of these credits is also outside the marketing budget.

Two creative angles should lead. The first is "your invoice template is the problem", shown as a split-screen of a messy Word or Excel invoice next to the Tallyho invoice screen. The second is "your Belastingdienst deadline is coming", using the end-of-January BTW return as a reason to switch now. The exact deadline date should be checked before it appears in copy.

## 5. Budget allocation

The budget is EUR 15,000 in total. Roughly 73 percent goes to channels that produce paid signups directly, and the remainder covers content, measurement, and a contingency reserve.

| Line | Budget (EUR) | Share | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meta paid social (prospecting and retargeting) | 4,000 | 27% | Test broad Dutch audiences, then scale winning ad sets |
| Google Search (high-intent Dutch terms) | 3,000 | 20% | Capture people already searching for invoice tools and templates |
| Creators and micro-influencers (about 6 Dutch zzp voices) | 3,000 | 20% | Flat fees plus affiliate codes, clearly labelled as advertising |
| Partner program (accountants and bookkeepers) | 1,500 | 10% | Performance commission of about EUR 30 per paid signup |
| Content and SEO (Word and Excel invoice templates, Dutch guides) | 2,000 | 13% | Long-term organic traffic, mostly after the campaign |
| Measurement, email tooling, and landing page tests | 500 | 3% | Attribution, consent tooling, and page testing |
| Contingency | 1,000 | 7% | Released only when a channel proves itself |

Phasing matters. About EUR 11,000 should be spent during the six-week sprint and about EUR 4,000 during the sustain phase to 2027-03-31. Paid social and search should start at a test level of about EUR 150 per day in total in week 1, then move to scale only when the kill and scale rules in section 8 are met.

## 6. Channel plan and forecast

The forecast below is a planning estimate. It rests on the assumptions in section 2 and should be updated every week with actual data.

| Channel | Assumed paid signups by 2027-03-31 | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Waitlist (launch email, offer, follow-ups) | 720 (range 500 to 1,000) | 18 percent of 4,000 pay |
| Referrals from paying customers | 150 | Driven by the free-month mechanic |
| Partners (accountants and bookkeepers) | 50 | Webinars and co-branded templates at about EUR 30 each |
| Creators | 50 | About EUR 60 per paid signup on the creator budget |
| Google Search and Meta | 80 | About EUR 90 blended cost per paid signup on EUR 7,000 |
| Organic search and content | 80 | Slow ramp, mostly from the second month onward |
| Total base case | 1,130 | About EUR 13 per paid signup across all spend |

On a base case of 1,130 paid signups at EUR 12 per month, monthly recurring revenue by the end of March would be about EUR 13,500 before churn. That is short of the 1,500 target, and the gap of about 370 signups would cost roughly EUR 33,000 at the EUR 90 assumed for paid media. This is why the plan treats the goal as at risk, not as a commitment.

Channel detail follows.

**Waitlist.** This is the highest-yield channel and should get the most care. Segment the waitlist by how each person invoices today (Word, Excel, or another tool), and send a sequence of about five emails. The sequence starts with early access 14 days before launch, then the launch email on 2027-01-12, a reminder after 5 days, a deadline email on 2027-02-25 for the offer ending 2027-02-28, and a last-chance message. Track opens, clicks, trial starts, and paid conversions per segment, so that the copy can be improved for each group.

**Referrals.** Build a simple referral link into the account area and into the onboarding email. Keep the mechanic to one clear reward so that it is easy to explain in a single sentence.

**Partners.** Approach 20 to 30 accountants and bookkeepers who serve zzp clients. Offer a co-branded invoice template, a 45-minute webinar on invoicing for freelancers, and a commission of about EUR 30 per paid signup. Partners often bring the most loyal customers, so this channel deserves more attention than its budget suggests.

**Creators.** Choose about six Dutch creators who speak to freelancers, on YouTube, LinkedIn, or Instagram. Each creator gets a flat fee and an affiliate code, and each post is labelled as advertising under the Reclame Code rules. Ask for a demonstration of the real invoicing flow rather than a generic product mention.

**Google Search.** Target Dutch terms such as "factuur maken", "factuursjabloon excel", "factuur maken in word", and "factuurprogramma zzp". I do not have a verified Dutch cost-per-click figure, so the first two weeks should be used to measure it. Use exact and phrase match at the start, and add negative keywords for free-only and job-seeking searches.

**Meta.** Start with broad Dutch audiences aged roughly 25 to 55, interests around freelancing and entrepreneurship, and Advantage+ placements. Use retargeting for site visitors and trial users. Do not build lookalike audiences until there are at least around 300 paying customers to seed them, because a lookalike on a list that small performs poorly. Creatives should be short, with the split-screen angle at the top.

**Content and SEO.** Publish free Word and Excel invoice templates with a clear path into Tallyho, and a small set of guides such as how to make a compliant invoice in Word. These pages take months to rank, so most of their value falls after 2027-03-31. They are still worth starting now because the templates also feed the waitlist and social channels.

**Founder and LinkedIn.** Post weekly from the founder account about what is changing in invoicing for freelancers. This costs little, supports the creator and partner work, and builds trust with the audience.

## 7. Options for closing the gap

The plan cannot reach 1,500 paid signups on the current budget, so the team has three real choices, and each should be decided before 2027-01-12.

1. Accept a base case of about 1,100 to 1,200 paid signups by 2027-03-31, and treat 1,500 as a target for the end of Q2 2027.
2. Raise the paid media budget by about EUR 25,000 to EUR 30,000 for the sustain period. This only makes sense if cost per paid signup holds under EUR 90 once scaled, which the first six weeks will show.
3. Increase waitlist conversion through a stronger early-access reward, such as a founding member price that is locked for life. This is the cheapest lever, but it reduces revenue per customer.

My recommendation is option 1 with a decision gate on 2027-02-15. If cost per paid signup is under EUR 90 and waitlist conversion is above 15 percent by that date, option 2 becomes worth considering.

## 8. Measurement, rules, and cadence

Track the funnel in four stages: waitlist click, trial start, paid conversion, and retained customer at 30 days. Use one UTM convention for every link, and send paid events from the server through the Meta conversion API as well as the browser pixel, because browser tracking will lose a share of conversions. Consent for tracking and for waitlist email must follow the AVG and ePrivacy rules, so the consent banner needs to be live before any pixel fires.

Use these rules, which are starting points and should be adjusted once real data arrives:

- Pause any ad set whose cost per trial start is above EUR 30 after EUR 250 of spend.
- Scale an ad set once it has produced at least 20 paid signups at a cost under EUR 90.
- Stop a creator partnership if it produces no trial starts within 14 days of its post.
- Review the whole plan every Monday, with the budget split updated against actual cost per paid signup.

The weekly report should show spend, trial starts, paid signups, and cost per paid signup for each channel, plus the running total against the 1,500 target. The number to watch is the cumulative paid count against the forecast curve, not the daily noise in ad metrics.

## 10. Risks

The largest risk is that the waitlist is less warm than assumed. If it was collected a long time ago, or through a generic signup, conversion could fall well below 18 percent, and the base case would fall with it. The first email will show this within a day, so the team should be ready to shift budget toward partners and creators quickly.

The second risk is that paid media costs more than assumed. With no ad history and a low price, cost per paid signup could sit above EUR 150. The kill rules in section 8 limit the damage, but they also mean the paid channels may contribute less than the forecast shows.

The third risk is claims. Price comparisons, time savings, and compliance statements all need evidence. A comparison that is wrong by the time the ad runs is a bigger problem than a weaker ad.

The fourth risk is product readiness. The plan assumes onboarding works smoothly for people switching from Word or Excel, including importing existing client lists. If that is not true on 2027-01-12, the paid signups will churn quickly, and the figure to watch becomes retention at 30 days, not signups alone.

## 11. Immediate next steps

Before 2027-01-11, the team should confirm the budget scope and the offer, verify the waitlist consent status and segmentation, install the tracking and conversion API, check the competitor prices on each vendor's live page, confirm the BTW deadline date for the messaging, recruit the first creators and partners, and agree the decision gate on 2027-02-15.
