# Briefing: Linear's free tier for teams of up to 10, and what Trackwise should do in the next 30 days

Date: 2026-10-18

## Assumptions

I have taken the launch as given: Linear now offers a free plan for teams of up to 10 people. I do not yet know its limits (issue caps, guest access, integrations, history), so the first task below is to find them out. I have also assumed that Trackwise is billed per seat per month with no free plan today, that it has a time-limited trial, and that a meaningful share of our customers sit at the small end of our 5 to 30 person range. Before this launch, Linear's paid plans were reported at about USD 10 per user per month (Basic) and USD 16 (Business) on annual billing, and its earlier free plan was capped at around 250 issues ([usagepricing.com](https://usagepricing.com/blueprint/linear), [automationatlas.io](https://automationatlas.io/answers/linear-pricing-2026/)). Check those figures against Linear's own pricing page before using them in any customer-facing material.

## The short version

This matters, but it is not an emergency. The threat is real for agencies of 5 to 10 people, where Linear now costs nothing and Trackwise costs USD 40 to 80 a month. It is weak for agencies of 11 to 30 people, where Linear is now more expensive than us per seat and still lacks the two things agencies pay us for: client portals and time tracking. Our response should be to protect the small accounts we already have, make the "total cost of the agency stack" argument loudly and clearly, and position Trackwise as the tool an agency moves to when it outgrows Linear's free tier. We should not cut our price or launch a free plan in a panic. We should collect data for 30 days and then make a pricing decision based on evidence.

## What this means for us

**1. New-customer acquisition at the small end gets harder.** A 6-person agency choosing its first issue tracker will now see "free" next to a well-known, well-designed brand. Free is a strong anchor even when the amount saved is small (USD 48 a month at 6 seats). Expect fewer trial sign-ups and a lower trial-to-paid rate among agencies under 10 people. This is the most likely and most immediate effect.

**2. Our smallest existing customers are a churn risk at renewal.** Customers with 10 or fewer seats now have a free alternative to point to. Most will not leave immediately, because switching tools costs time, but some will raise it at renewal or ask for a discount. Price-sensitive accounts and accounts that use few of our agency features (no portal use, little time tracking) are the most exposed.

**3. Above 10 seats, our position is unchanged or slightly better.** An 11-person agency on Linear pays for every seat, at roughly USD 10 to 16 each versus our USD 8. On top of that it still needs a separate time-tracking tool and some workaround for sharing progress with clients (guest accounts, exported reports, or a separate portal product). Our integrated offer is cheaper and simpler for that buyer.

**4. Linear is creating a "cliff" we can use.** The free tier is a land-and-expand move: agencies start on Linear for free and are expected to pay once they pass 10 people. The moment an agency hires its 11th person, its bill goes from zero to a full per-seat charge in one step. That is a natural moment to reconsider tools, and we want to be the obvious alternative at that moment.

**5. Our differentiation becomes the whole argument.** When a competitor is free, "we are a good issue tracker" stops being a reason to pay. Client portals and time tracking, and the fact that they come in one tool built for how agencies bill clients, are what we must lead with everywhere.

**6. The longer-term risk is Linear moving into our niche.** If Linear adds client-facing views or time tracking, our differentiation narrows. There is no sign of that in this launch, but we should watch their changelog.

## How we should respond in the next 30 days

### Days 1 to 5: get the facts and size the exposure

- **Read Linear's free-tier terms in full.** Find out the seat limit, any issue or team caps, whether guests or external users count as seats, which integrations are included, and what happens at seat 11. Every message we write depends on these details.
- **Segment our customer base by seat count.** Pull the number of accounts and the revenue at 5 to 10 seats, 11 to 20, and 21 to 30. Flag accounts at 10 or fewer seats whose renewal falls in the next 90 days. This tells us how much revenue is actually at risk. If the 10-and-under segment is a small share of revenue, the response can be lighter.
- **Check feature usage in the at-risk group.** Accounts that use client portals and time tracking heavily are much less likely to leave than accounts that use Trackwise only as an issue list. Use this to prioritise outreach.
- **Brief the team.** Give sales and support a one-page internal note: what Linear launched, how we compare, and how to answer "Why should I pay when Linear is free?" Ask them to log every mention of Linear in calls, tickets and cancellation reasons from today.

### Days 5 to 15: protect existing customers

- **Reach out first to small, at-risk accounts.** Have customer success contact the flagged accounts personally before they raise the issue. The message is not "please don't leave" but a value check-in: show them how much time they have tracked, how many client portal views they have had, and offer help setting up features they do not use yet.
- **Make the total-cost case concrete.** For a typical 8-person agency, compare Trackwise (USD 64 a month, everything included) with Linear free plus a separate time-tracking tool plus a client reporting workaround. Use real list prices for the time-tracking tools agencies commonly use, and verify them before publishing. Include the cost of switching and the cost of the cliff at seat 11.
- **Offer a renewal incentive, not a price cut.** For small accounts that push back, an annual-commitment discount or an extra month free is safer than lowering the list price. It protects revenue and avoids teaching the whole base to ask for discounts.

### Days 10 to 25: sharpen positioning and capture switchers

- **Update the website and the comparison page.** Lead with "the issue tracker built for agencies that bill clients" and put client portals and time tracking above the fold. Publish an honest Trackwise vs Linear page: acknowledge that Linear is an excellent tool for product teams, then explain why agencies need more. Do not attack Linear; it is well liked and attacks look defensive.
- **Build a "when you outgrow Linear free" path.** Create a short guide and landing page aimed at agencies near or past 10 people. Make sure we have (or quickly build) a Linear import so switching takes minutes, and say so prominently. Consider a switching offer, such as the first three months at a reduced rate or free migration help, for agencies coming from Linear.
- **Target the right audience in paid and organic channels.** Focus spend and content on agencies of roughly 8 to 30 people and on searches about agency client reporting, client portals and billable time, not on generic "issue tracker" or "Linear alternative" terms where we are competing against "free".
- **Lengthen the trial as a quick, reversible test.** If our trial is 14 days, try 30 days for new sign-ups. Small agencies need a full client cycle to feel the value of portals and time tracking. This is easy to reverse if it does not help.

### Days 25 to 30: decide on pricing with evidence

By day 30 we should have data on trial sign-ups, trial conversion, renewal outcomes in the 10-and-under segment, and how often Linear comes up in sales and churn conversations. Use it to choose between three options:

1. **Hold the current pricing.** This is right if churn and conversion in the small segment have barely moved, which is quite possible given our differentiation.
2. **Launch a limited free or starter plan.** For example, up to 3 seats with one client portal, so that very small agencies can start with us and grow into paid. This competes with Linear's anchor without giving away our core 5 to 30 market.
3. **Introduce a small-agency bundle.** For example, a flat monthly price for up to 10 seats with all agency features included, so the comparison is a simple, low, predictable number rather than a per-seat calculation.

My recommendation is to prepare options 2 and 3 in parallel during the month but to commit to nothing until the day-30 review. Matching Linear on "free for 10" would give away a large part of our core segment's revenue to defend against a threat whose size we have not yet measured.

## What to measure

- Trial sign-ups and trial-to-paid conversion, split by agency size, compared with the previous 30 days.
- Renewal rate and churn among accounts with 10 or fewer seats.
- The number of sales calls, support tickets and cancellation reasons that mention Linear.
- Sign-ups and conversions from the comparison page, the "outgrow Linear" page and the switching offer.
- Use of client portals and time tracking in new accounts, as a leading sign of retention.

## Risks to watch

- **Linear adds agency features.** If they ship client views or time tracking, our differentiation narrows and we need to move faster on depth (invoicing hooks, client approvals, budget tracking).
- **Discount creep.** Ad-hoc discounts to save small accounts can spread across the base. Keep incentives structured and time-limited.
- **Over-reacting.** A rushed free plan or price cut is hard to reverse. The 30-day measurement window exists to prevent that.
