# Tallyho trial onboarding email sequence

## Assumptions

I made these assumptions because the brief does not cover them. Change them if they are wrong.

- The trial lasts 14 days, no card is needed to start, and the account stops sending invoices at the end of day 14 unless the user adds a card. Data is kept for at least 30 days after the trial ends.
- The price is EUR 12 per month excluding btw, billed monthly, and the user can cancel at any time. If you also sell an annual plan, add it to emails T3 and T4.
- Your email tool can trigger an email when an event happens ("first invoice sent", "card added") and can stop a track when the user moves to another track. Any common lifecycle tool can do this, for example Customer.io, Loops, Intercom or HubSpot.
- The emails go out in Dutch. I wrote the copy in English so it can be reviewed, and it should be localised, not translated word for word. Dutch freelancers write informally ("je", not "u"), so the tone is direct and friendly.
- The sender is a named person at Tallyho, for example the founder or the support lead. Replies go to a real inbox that someone reads within one working day.
- Placeholders are in square brackets, for example [first_name], [trial_end_date] and [invoice_client_name].

## What the numbers tell us

Today 38% of trial users send an invoice and 11% pay. If almost everyone who pays has sent an invoice first, about 29% of activated users convert (11 ÷ 38) and almost no one who never sent an invoice converts. That makes activation the main lever, and it gives the sequence one job in week one: get the first invoice sent.

A rough model of the upside, assuming the 29% activated-to-paid rate stays the same:

| Activation rate | Expected trial-to-paid |
|---|---|
| 38% (today) | 11% |
| 45% | 13% |
| 50% | 14.5% |
| 55% | 16% |

The second lever is the end of the trial. Because no card is needed at sign-up, every conversion needs the user to come back and add a card. Activated users who forget are lost revenue, so the last four days need clear deadline reminders for that group.

There is also a timing problem that is specific to invoicing. Most freelancers invoice once a month, often at the end of the month. A user who signs up on the 5th may have no real invoice to send during a 14-day trial. Some of the 62% who never activate are likely in this situation; they are waiting, not lost. The sequence deals with this in three ways: it suggests scheduling the next invoice now, it suggests sending a small real invoice that is already due (for example a late expense or a deposit), and it offers a one-click trial extension.

## How the sequence works

There are two tracks. Every user starts on Track A. When a user sends their first invoice, they leave Track A at once and join Track B. When a user adds a card, they leave both tracks and get only the "you are subscribed" email.

**Track A, not yet activated.** Every email has one goal: send the first invoice. Each email deals with a different reason why people do not send.

**Track B, activated.** The goal is to show the value that comes after the first invoice (getting paid, reminders, btw overview) and then to ask for the card before the deadline.

Both tracks share the trial-end emails, with different copy for each track.

### Overview

| ID | Track | When | Goal | Subject line (English draft) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | A | Sign-up, immediately | Start the first invoice | Your first invoice is 3 minutes away |
| A2 | A | Day 1, if no invoice | Remove the "is it correct?" worry | Everything a Dutch invoice needs (Tallyho checks it for you) |
| A3 | A | Day 3, if no invoice | Fix "I have nothing to invoice yet" | No invoice due yet? Schedule it now |

| A6 | A | Day 11, if no invoice | Offer more time | Want two more weeks? |
| B1 | B | First invoice sent, immediately | Celebrate and show the next step | Invoice sent to [invoice_client_name] |
| B2 | B | 2 days after B1 | Show payment tracking and reminders | You do not have to chase payments any more |
| B3 | B | 5 days after B1, if before day 11 | Show the btw overview and repeat use | Your btw return just got shorter |

| T2 | Both | Day 13 | Deadline reminder | Your trial ends tomorrow |

| T4 | Both | Day 17, if no card | Win back | Your invoices are still here |
| S1 | All | Card added | Confirm and reassure | You are all set |

Rules that apply to all emails:

- A user gets at most one email per day. When a Track A or Track B email and a T email fall on the same day, the T email wins.
- B3 is skipped if it would land on day 11 or later, because the trial-end emails take over.
- A6 is skipped for users who already asked for an extension.
- Every email has one main call to action. A plain-text P.S. may carry a second, softer one.
- Every email is short enough to read on a phone without scrolling much. A1, A4 and T3 are written as plain text from a person. The others can use a light template with the Tallyho logo and a single button.

## Track A: not yet activated

### A1. Welcome (sent immediately at sign-up)

**Subject:** Your first invoice is 3 minutes away
**Preview text:** Add one client, add one line, click send.

Hi [first_name],

Welcome to Tallyho. Your trial is open for 14 days, until [trial_end_date], and you do not need a card.

The best way to see whether Tallyho suits you is to send one real invoice. It takes about three minutes:

1. Add your business details once: your name, address, KvK number and btw-id. Tallyho puts them on every invoice from now on.
2. Add the client you are invoicing next.
3. Add what you did and your rate, then click send.

Tallyho gives the invoice a number, works out the btw and sends a PDF to your client with a link to pay.

[Button: Create my first invoice]

If anything is unclear, reply to this email. I read every reply myself.

[Sender name]
[Role], Tallyho

P.S. Do you use the kleineondernemersregeling (KOR)? Switch it on in your settings and Tallyho leaves btw off your invoices and adds the correct note.

**Why it works:** One action and one button, and the three steps make the job feel small. The P.S. answers a common Dutch freelancer question before it becomes a reason to stop.

### A2. "Is it correct?" (day 1, no invoice yet)

**Subject:** Everything a Dutch invoice needs (Tallyho checks it for you)
**Preview text:** Invoice number, btw-id, KvK number and more. You do not have to remember them.

Hi [first_name],

Many freelancers put off their invoices because they are not sure they have everything the Belastingdienst wants on them. That is a fair worry: a missing detail can mean your client sends the invoice back.

A Dutch invoice must show, among other things:

- a unique invoice number that follows on from the last one
- the invoice date and the date of the work
- your name, address, KvK number and btw-id
- your client's name and address
- a description of the work, the amount without btw, the btw rate and the btw amount

Tallyho fills in all of these for you and warns you if something is missing before you send.

[Button: Finish my first invoice]

[Sender name]

**Design note:** If the user already started a draft, change the button to "Finish my draft for [draft_client_name]". Unfinished drafts are the easiest activations to win.

### A3. "Nothing to invoice yet" (day 3, no invoice yet)

**Subject:** No invoice due yet? Schedule it now
**Preview text:** Set it up today and Tallyho sends it on the day you choose.

Hi [first_name],

Most freelancers invoice at the end of the month, so you may not have anything to bill during your trial. Here are two ways to try Tallyho properly anyway:

**Schedule your next invoice.** Create the invoice now, pick the date you want it to go out, and Tallyho sends it on that day. If you bill the same client every month, make it a repeating invoice and you will not have to think about it again.

**Send a small invoice that is already due.** An expense you have not passed on yet, a deposit for a new project, or the extra hour from last week all count.

[Button: Schedule an invoice]

If your next invoice falls after your trial ends, reply with "more time" and I will extend your trial.

[Sender name]

**Measurement note:** Decide now whether a scheduled invoice counts as activation on the day it is created or on the day it is sent. I recommend tracking both: "invoice scheduled" as a leading signal, and "invoice sent" as the activation event the brief defines. A user with a scheduled invoice should get Track B copy that says "Your invoice to [client] goes out on [date]".

### A4. The personal question (day 5, no invoice yet)

Send this as plain text, with no template or images.

**Subject:** Quick question, [first_name]
**Preview text:** What is stopping you from sending your first invoice?

Hi [first_name],

I saw that you have not sent an invoice with Tallyho yet. That is fine; I would just like to know why, so we can make it easier.

Which of these comes closest? Just reply with the number.

1. I have no invoice to send right now.
2. I am still using another tool or a spreadsheet and do not want to switch mid-month.
3. Something in Tallyho was confusing or missing.
4. I am just looking around for now.

Thanks,
[Sender name]

**Why it works:** Replies give you the real blockers in the user's own words, and a personal email usually gets far more replies than a template. Answer each reply by hand at first. Reply 1 gets a trial extension offer. Reply 2 gets help with moving clients and the next invoice number across (see A5). Reply 3 goes to the product team. Tag each answer in your email tool so you can count them after a month.

### A5. The payoff (day 8, no invoice yet)

**Subject:** What happens after you click send
**Preview text:** Your client gets a pay link, and you see when it is paid.

Hi [first_name],

Here is what one invoice in Tallyho looks like from start to finish:

- **Day 0:** You send the invoice. Your client gets a clear PDF and a payment link (iDEAL works).
- **When the client opens it:** You see that it has been opened, so you know it did not land in spam.
- **When the payment term ends:** If it has not been paid, Tallyho sends a polite reminder for you. You choose the wording once.
- **When it is paid:** The invoice is marked as paid and appears in your btw overview for the quarter.

Moving from another tool or a spreadsheet? Import your clients from a CSV file and set your next invoice number so your numbering keeps running without a gap.

[Button: Send my first invoice]

[Sender name]

**Note for the team:** Only promise features Tallyho actually has. If you do not offer iDEAL payment links, open tracking or automatic reminders, remove those lines and lead with what you do offer.

### A6. Extension offer (day 11, no invoice yet)

**Subject:** Want two more weeks?
**Preview text:** One click, no card needed.

Hi [first_name],

Your trial ends on [trial_end_date], and you have not sent an invoice yet. If that is because your next invoice is not due yet, I would rather you test Tallyho with a real client than decide without trying it.

[Button: Give me 14 more days]

If you have already decided Tallyho is not for you, no problem. A one-line reply about why would help us a lot.

[Sender name]

**Why it works:** It turns the most common timing problem into a second chance. Limit it to one extension per account and track how many extended users activate. If that rate is low, the extension is not worth it and you can drop this email.

## How to measure it

Main metrics, measured per weekly sign-up group:

- **Activation rate:** the share of trials that send an invoice within 14 days (today 38%).
- **Trial-to-paid rate:** the share of trials that add a card within 21 days of sign-up (today 11%).
- **Activated-to-paid rate:** the share of activated users who pay (today about 29%, estimated).

Supporting metrics:

- Time from sign-up to first invoice, as a median in hours.
- Share of trials with a scheduled or draft invoice that is never sent.
- Extension requests, and the activation rate of extended users.
- A4 reply rate, and the split of answers across the four reasons.
- Click rate per email. Do not rely on open rates; Apple Mail privacy protection inflates them.

To prove that the sequence works, keep 10% of new trials as a holdout group that gets only A1, T2 and S1. Compare activation and trial-to-paid rates after about 400 trials per group. Below that size, a gain of a few points is hard to tell apart from noise.

## What to test first

Run these one at a time, in this order, because each one targets the biggest open question:

1. **Extension offer in A3 and A6 compared with no extension offer.** This tests whether timing is the main reason people do not activate.
2. **Plain-text A1 compared with a designed A1.** The welcome email gets the most attention of the sequence.
3. **T2 Version B with the open-invoice amount compared with without it.** This tests whether loss framing gets more activated users to add a card.

## What the sequence does not fix

Emails can only bring people back to the product. If the A4 replies show that people get stuck in the product itself, for example during business setup or when they first add a client, fix the product first. Two in-app changes are likely to help as much as the emails: a setup checklist that ends with "Send your first invoice", and the option to send a test invoice to your own email address before sending to a client.
