# CleanPlate Win-Back Email Sequence

**Audience:** Customers who cancelled their CleanPlate subscription in the last 90 days
**Goal:** Reactivate lapsed subscribers and keep them past the discounted boxes
**Offer budget:** Up to 30% off two boxes
**New product levers:** 2-person half-portions and one-click skip

---

## Assumptions

I could not confirm a few details, so I have assumed the following. Change them if any are wrong.

1. **Consent.** Everyone in the audience still has valid marketing consent and has not unsubscribed. Cancelling the subscription did not remove their email permission.
2. **Exit survey data.** Each customer's cancel reason is stored on their profile in the email platform, so the reason can be used for segmentation. Customers who skipped the survey go into the "Other / unknown" track.
3. **Half-portions.** The "2-person half-portion" plan gives smaller servings for two people, so there is less food left over. I have also assumed it costs less per box than the standard plan. If it does not, remove the price line from the price track.
4. **One-click skip.** Customers can skip any upcoming week with one tap, from the app or from a reminder email, without having to log in to a settings page.
5. **Offer mechanics.** "30% off two boxes" means 30% off each of the customer's first two boxes after they reactivate. The discount applies automatically through a personalised link, and the customer can also enter a code. After box two, the normal price applies.
6. **Pricing placeholders.** I do not know CleanPlate's prices, so I have used placeholders such as `[standard box price]` and `[half-portion box price]`.
7. **Brand voice.** I have written in a warm, plain, lightly playful voice that does not guilt-trip. Adjust it to match the brand guide.

---

## Strategy in brief

The exit survey shows that most people did not leave because they disliked the food. They left because of **cost**, **waste** and **hassle**. CleanPlate has now fixed two of these problems with product changes, and the discount addresses the third. So the sequence has one main message: **"The thing that made you leave has changed."**

Five principles shape the sequence:

1. **Lead with the reason they left.** Each customer's first email speaks to the reason they gave in the exit survey. The other two fixes appear later as supporting points, because most people have more than one frustration.
2. **Use the discount where it does the most work.** Price-driven churners (45%) get the 30% offer in the first email, because the offer is the answer to their objection. For leftovers and skipping churners, the product fix is the answer, so they see the fix first and the offer in the second email. This keeps the offer from hiding the real news and avoids teaching people to wait for a discount.
3. **Make coming back feel low-risk.** Every email repeats that customers can skip, pause or cancel in one click. Many people who cancelled were really looking for a way to take a break, so showing the easy way out makes coming back feel safe.
4. **Plan for box three.** A discount that brings people back for two boxes and then loses them again is a loss. The sequence includes a post-reactivation email that prepares customers for full price and points them to the plan that suits them.
5. **Stop when it stops working.** The sequence has four emails over about two weeks, plus one follow-up after the offer ends. After that, non-responders move to a low-frequency newsletter.

## Segmentation

### By cancel reason (which track each customer follows)

| Track | Share | Main message | Offer timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Price | 45% | "Come back for less: 30% off two boxes, and a new smaller plan that costs less" | Email 1 |
| B. Too many leftovers | 30% | "New 2-person half-portions: the right amount of food" | Email 2 |
| C. Skipping was too hard | 15% | "Skip any week in one click" | Email 2 |
| D. Other / unknown | 10% | "A lot has changed": all three fixes, plus a one-question survey | Email 2 |

### By time since cancellation (when customers enter)

- **Initial backlog send (everyone who cancelled 14 to 90 days ago):** Split them into three waves by recency (14 to 30, 31 to 60 and 61 to 90 days) and send to the most recent group first. This gives early results and protects sender reputation.
- **Customers who cancelled 0 to 13 days ago:** Hold them until day 14 after cancellation. Most of them have just received their last box or recently said no to an exit offer, so emailing too soon feels pushy.
- **Ongoing:** After the backlog is done, keep the flow running as an automation that starts 14 days after each new cancellation.

### Exclusions and exit rules

Do not send to:
- Customers who have unsubscribed from marketing emails or marked a CleanPlate email as spam.
- Customers who cancelled because CleanPlate does not deliver to their area, or who now live outside the delivery zone.
- Customers with an open complaint, an unresolved refund or a recent food-safety ticket. Send them a personal service follow-up instead.
- Accounts closed for payment fraud or chargebacks.
- Customers who got a retention discount of 25% or more in the 30 days before they cancelled. Send these customers the sequence without the discount, so CleanPlate does not train them to cancel in order to get offers.

Remove a customer from the sequence when they:
- Reactivate (move them to the "Welcome back" email below).
- Unsubscribe.
- Reply to the one-question survey and choose "I'm not coming back." Send a short, gracious acknowledgement and suppress them for 90 days.

### Holdout group

Keep a random **10% of each track** out of the sequence entirely. Without a holdout, there is no way to know how many of these people would have come back on their own, and the reactivation rate will look better than it really is.

## Sequence overview

| # | Day | Purpose | Track A: Price | Tracks B / C / D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | "We listened" | Fix + 30% offer | Fix only, no discount |
| 2 | 4 | Offer | Show what the customer would pay, with menu | 30% offer introduced |
| 3 | 9 | Menu and proof | Menu, reviews and all three fixes, plus offer reminder | Same |
| 4 | 14 | Last chance | Offer ends in 48 hours | Same |
| 5 | 30 | Soft check-in | One question, no discount | Same |
| Post | After reactivation | Welcome back, then prepare for box 3 | Same for all | Same for all |

Send times: Use the customer's past order-day pattern if you have it. Otherwise send on Thursday or Sunday mornings, when people usually plan the week's meals.

The offer expires **16 days after Email 1** (which allows 48 hours after Email 4), so the deadline is real.

## Post-reactivation emails (protecting box 3)

The real test of a win-back is whether people stay after the discount ends. These emails replace the rest of the sequence as soon as someone reactivates.

### "Welcome back" (sent immediately)

**Subject:** "Welcome back, {{first_name}}. Here's your first box."
**Content:** Delivery date for the first box. A reminder of the plan they chose. A one-line tip about the feature that matters to them (for example, "Your weekly reminder arrives every [day]. Tap 'Skip' if you don't need a box."). A link to change meals.

### "Make it fit" (sent 2 days after box 2 is delivered)

**Subject:** "Your next box is full price. Here's how to make it fit."
**Content:**
- Say clearly that the discount has ended and give the exact price of the next box and the date it will be charged. Being upfront here earns trust. A surprise full-price charge is one of the fastest ways to lose a returning customer and to get a chargeback.
- For Track A customers on the standard plan, suggest switching to half-portions if that lowers their cost.
- Remind everyone of one-click skip: "Not this week? Skip it before [cut-off]."

## Offer guardrails

- **One win-back offer per customer every 6 months.** Track this on the customer's profile.
- **Personal, single-use links and codes,** so the offer cannot spread on coupon sites.
- **Margin check:** Before launch, confirm that 30% off two boxes, multiplied by the expected box 3 retention rate, still pays back within an acceptable period. If price-track margins are tight, test a 20% cell against 30% in Track A.
- **New-customer offers:** If CleanPlate's new-customer offer is larger than 30%, some lapsed customers will try to sign up again with a new email address. Make sure the new-customer offer checks for previous accounts by address and payment method.

---

## Testing plan

Test one thing at a time, and run each test long enough to reach a reliable sample.

1. **Offer timing for Tracks B and C (most important test):** Fix first, offer in Email 2 (as designed) versus offer in Email 1. Measure reactivation rate *and* box 3 retention. Fix-first may reactivate fewer people but keep more of them.
2. **Offer depth for Track A:** 30% versus 20% off two boxes. Measure reactivation, box 3 retention and net revenue per recipient.
3. **Subject lines:** Test the A and B options in Email 1 for each track.
4. **Plan default for Track B:** Link to a half-portion plan pre-selected versus a neutral plan page.

---

## Measurement

Measure every metric against the 10% holdout, and report it by track and by recency wave.

**Primary metric**
- **Incremental reactivation rate:** the reactivation rate of the group that got the emails minus the rate of the holdout group.

**Quality metrics (as important as the primary metric)**
- **Box 3 retention:** the share of reactivated customers who take a full-price third box.
- **60-day and 90-day retention** after reactivation.
- **Net revenue per recipient** after discount cost, compared with the holdout.
- **Plan mix:** how many returning customers chose half-portions, and how many used one-click skip in their first month. If returning skip-track customers use skip often but stay subscribed, the feature is working.

**Health metrics**
- Unsubscribe rate per email (investigate if it is above about 0.5%).
- Spam complaint rate (stop and review if it is above 0.1%).
- Survey response rate and answers from Track D and Email 5.

**Rough expectations:** I do not have CleanPlate's past data, so I am not giving a benchmark reactivation rate here. Use the first backlog wave (14 to 30 days since cancellation) to set a baseline. Expect that group to perform best and the 61 to 90 day group to perform worst.
