## Blog post

### The Ferrous Postgres CDC connector is generally available

Today the Ferrous Postgres change-data-capture (CDC) connector is generally available. It reads changes from the Postgres write-ahead log and streams every insert, update and delete to your downstream systems as they happen. You can run it yourself, because it is open source under the Apache-2.0 licence, or you can use the managed version on Ferrous Cloud.

This post explains what the connector does well, how we measured it, and how to start.

### Why change data capture

Most teams first move data out of Postgres with scheduled batch jobs. Batch jobs are simple, but they add delay, they put load on the primary database, and they miss deletes unless you design for them. CDC takes a different path. It reads the changes that Postgres already records in its log, so downstream systems see each change once, in order, shortly after it commits. Caches stay fresh, search indexes stay in step, and analytics tables reflect what is true now instead of what was true last night.

### Sub-second latency

Freshness is the reason to adopt CDC, so we measured it end to end: from the moment a transaction commits in Postgres to the moment the change is available downstream. In our benchmark, the connector held a p95 latency of 420 ms at a sustained 20,000 rows per second. That means 95 percent of changes arrived in less than half a second while the database was under steady write load.

Benchmarks depend on their setup, and yours will differ. We have published the full method, configuration and scripts at [benchmark link] so that you can reproduce the test on your own hardware and workload.

### No replication-slot bloat

Postgres CDC relies on a replication slot. The slot tells Postgres which parts of the write-ahead log a consumer still needs, and Postgres keeps those log files until the consumer confirms it has processed them. This is what makes CDC reliable. It is also the cause of one of its best-known failures.

When the tables you capture go quiet but other activity on the server keeps writing to the log, the connector has nothing new to confirm. The slot stops advancing, the log keeps growing, and eventually the disk fills. Many teams have learned about this problem from a production alert.

The Ferrous connector prevents it with slot heartbeats. At a regular interval, the connector produces a small amount of activity that it can confirm, so the slot keeps moving forward even when your captured tables are idle. Postgres can then release log files it no longer needs, and disk use stays flat. You do not need a separate cron job or a custom script to keep the slot healthy.

### Open source under Apache-2.0

The connector is open source under the Apache-2.0 licence. You can read every line, run it in your own environment, and adapt it to your stack without legal surprises. We develop it in the open at [GitHub link], and we welcome issues and pull requests.
