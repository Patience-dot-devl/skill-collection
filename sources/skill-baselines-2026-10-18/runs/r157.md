# Tallyho Trial Onboarding Email Sequence

## Assumptions

These assumptions shape the sequence. Change them if the facts differ.

- Tallyho is a web invoicing app for Dutch freelancers (zzp'ers and eenmanszaak owners). It costs EUR 12 per month.
- The trial lasts 14 days from signup. No card is needed to start, so the trial ends without a charge and the user has to choose to pay.
- Activation means the user has sent their first invoice to a client.
- The current funnel is 38% activation and 11% trial-to-paid. Because 11 divided by 38 is about 29%, the sequence assumes that most paying users come from the activated group. Conversion among users who never send an invoice is assumed to be close to zero. Check this against the data before you rely on it.
- The sender is a named person from the Tallyho team. Square-bracket placeholders such as [First name], [link] and [X days] need real values before sending.
- I did not verify three things. The required fields on a Dutch invoice, the data retention period after a trial, and the cancellation and VAT (BTW) terms of the EUR 12 price are all marked for checking. Confirm them before any email goes out.

## Strategy

Activation is the largest lever. Only 38% of trial users send an invoice, so 62% never reach the point where the product proves its value. The first seven days therefore focus on getting one invoice out. After activation, the emails shift to getting paid faster and to the decision to subscribe.

The sequence uses two tracks that split on activation status, plus a common start and a common end. Each email is sent only if the user is still in the right track on the send date. A user who converts to paid leaves the sequence at once.

| Day | Track | Email | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | All | E1 Welcome and first invoice | Start the activation path |
| 1 | Not activated | E2 Setup in two minutes | Remove setup friction |
| 3 | Not activated | E3 Stuck on something? | Surface the blocker |
| 5 | Activated | E4 What to do next | Build habit after first invoice |
| 7 | Not activated | E5 A shortcut | Offer a faster path to a first invoice |
| 7 | Activated | E6 Get paid faster | Show the payment features and preview the price |
| 10 | Activated, not paid | E7 Your own results | Make the value concrete and ask what would help |
| 12 | Not activated | E8 One invoice before it ends | Final activation attempt |
| 13 | Not paid | E9 Trial ends tomorrow | Make the subscription decision clear |
| 14 | Not paid | E10 Your trial has ended | Keep the door open and collect a reason |

## Exit rules

- Converted to paid at any point: stop the sequence and move the user to the paid onboarding track, which is outside this document.
- Unsubscribed or marked as spam: stop all emails immediately.
- Activated: skip E2, E3 and E5, and send E4, E6, E7 and E9 as written.
- Not activated: skip E4, E6 and E7, and send E2, E3, E5, E8 and E9 as written.

## Measurement

Track each email by its open rate, click rate and the action it targets. The main measures are as follows.

- **Activation rate by day 7.** The current rate is 38%. A reasonable first target is 45%. E2, E3 and E5 should increase it, and their performance should be measured separately.
- **Trial-to-paid rate.** The current rate is 11%. Track it separately for activated and non-activated users, so that you can see whether the activated group converts at about 29%.
- **Time to first invoice.** Measure the median time from signup to the first invoice. If it falls, the early emails are working.

Run one change at a time. Good first tests are the subject line of E1, the timing of E5 (day 7 or day 6), and the offer in E3 (reply or booking call).
