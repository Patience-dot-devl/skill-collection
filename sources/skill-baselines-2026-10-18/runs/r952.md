# Tallyho trial onboarding email sequence

## Assumptions

I made the following assumptions instead of asking. Change any of them and I will adjust the sequence.

- **Language.** The copy below is in English for review. Dutch freelancers should receive it in Dutch, or in the language they chose in the app. Local terms such as *factuur*, *btw*, *KvK* and *KOR* are kept where a Dutch freelancer would expect them.
- **Price.** EUR 12 per month, excluding btw, billed monthly, cancel any time. I have not invented an annual plan or a discount.
- **Trial mechanics.** The 14-day trial needs no card. When it ends without payment details, the account becomes read-only: the user can still see and download their invoices, but cannot send new ones. If Tallyho instead deletes data or keeps sending enabled, emails 9 and 10 need changing.
- **Product features.** I assumed Tallyho has the following: invoice templates that include the Dutch legal fields, automatic invoice numbering, btw rates of 21%, 9% and 0% plus a KOR setting, a payment link on the invoice (for example iDEAL), a "viewed" status, and automatic payment reminders. Anything Tallyho does not have must be removed from the copy before launch.
- **Brand voice.** No guidelines were given, so I used a clear, friendly and direct tone. The emails come from a named person at Tallyho (`{{sender_name}}`), because a personal sender suits a small product for freelancers.
- **Data.** I assumed that almost all paying customers sent at least one invoice during their trial. That means about 29% of activated trials pay (11 / 38), and close to 0% of non-activated trials pay. Check this in your own data first, because the whole strategy depends on it.

## 1. Sequence strategy

### What the numbers say

| Stage | Today | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Trial starts | 100% | |
| Sends first invoice (activation) | 38% | 62% of trials never reach the core value |
| Converts to paid | 11% | About 29% of activated users pay; non-activated users almost never pay |

The biggest lever is activation. If activation rises from 38% to 50% and the activated-to-paid rate stays at about 29%, paid conversion rises from 11% to about 14.5%, which is roughly 30% more new revenue from the same signups. The second lever is the activated-to-paid rate. Because no card is required, an activated user still has to take a separate step to add payment details, and many will simply let the trial lapse unless they are asked clearly at the right time.

The sequence therefore has two jobs:

1. **Get a first invoice sent, fast.** Every email to a non-activated user has one CTA: create and send an invoice.
2. **Turn an activated user into a paying one.** After the first invoice, show them the invoice working for them (viewed, paid, reminded), get a second invoice out, and then ask them to add payment details before day 14.

### Narrative arc

- **Track A (not yet activated):** "Your first invoice takes three minutes" → "Use an invoice you need to send anyway" → "The Dutch rules are already handled" → "What is stopping you? Reply and tell me."
- **Track B (activated):** "Your invoice is out, here is what happens next" → "Get paid faster and send the next one."
- **Trial end (everyone, with different copy per track):** "Four days left, here is what you keep" → "Tomorrow your trial ends" → "Your trial has ended, your invoices are safe."

### Journey mapping

| Email | Stage |
|---|---|
| 1 Welcome | Activation |
| 2 to 4 (Track A) | Activation, with objection handling |
| 5 to 6 (Track B) | Activation to habit (second invoice, getting paid) |
| 7 to 8 | Decision |
| 9 | Decision (last chance) |
| 10 | Recovery after the trial |

### Escalation logic

Track A moves from easy help (a three-minute task) to removing doubts (legal rules, existing Word template) to a direct personal question. Track B moves from celebration to deeper value to a clear request to pay. Urgency only appears in the final four days, and it is honest: the deadline is the real end of the trial.

### Success definition

- **Track A exits** when the user sends their first invoice. They then enter Track B.
- **The whole sequence exits** when the user adds payment details (converts to paid).

**Segment and condition notes:** Only users who have not sent an invoice. Route replies to a real, monitored inbox and tag them by reason (no client yet, btw question, missing feature, busy). The tags are the most useful research output of the whole sequence.

## 5. Branching logic notes

**Branching conditions**

- After email 1, 2 or 3: if the user has sent a real invoice, move them to Track B (email 5) and skip the rest of Track A.
- Email 6: only send if at least 3 trial days remain after email 5. Otherwise go straight to the trial-end emails.
- Email 7 and email 8: choose the version by activation status at the moment of sending.
- Day 14: activated users get email 9; never-activated users skip email 9 and get email 10 on day 17.
- If the user opened email 2 or 3 but did not click, keep the next email on schedule but test sending it at the user's usual app-login hour.

**Exit conditions**

- **Converted:** the user adds payment details and starts a paid subscription. Remove them at once from every email in this sequence and move them to a paid-customer welcome email.
- **Track A exit:** the user sends their first real invoice (not a test invoice). They move to Track B.
- **Sequence complete:** after email 9 (activated, unpaid) or after email 10 with no extension (never activated).

**Re-entry rules**

- One trial extension per account. A user who takes it re-enters at email 2, and the trial-end emails are rescheduled to the new end date.
- A user who signs up again with the same email address within 90 days does not get the welcome sequence again. Send them email 9 (reactivate) instead.
- Activated users who leave after email 9 enter a separate win-back sequence about 30 days later.

**Suppression rules**

- Do not send if the user has contacted support in the last 48 hours (except emails 8 and 9, which carry the trial deadline and should still go out).
- Do not send Track A emails if the user is in an active support conversation about setup.
- Respect marketing unsubscribes. Emails 8 and 9 contain account information (trial end, account status) and can be treated as service messages, but check this with whoever handles your privacy compliance under GDPR and Dutch rules.
- Never send more than one email from this sequence in the same 24 hours. If two are due, send the one later in the flow.

## 6. A/B test suggestions

1. **Welcome email CTA: "real invoice" against "test invoice to yourself first".**
   - Split: 50/50 of new signups for email 1.
   - Measure: share of users who send a real invoice within 72 hours. Do not judge by clicks, because the test-invoice CTA will get more clicks but may not lead to real invoices.
   - Run until each arm has about 400 or more signups, or for four weeks, whichever comes later.

2. **Email 4 format: plain-text personal note against designed email with a button.**
   - Split: 50/50 of users who reach email 4.
   - Measure: activation within 5 days of the email, plus reply rate. A plain-text email usually gets more replies, and the replies are valuable research.

3. **Email 7 framing (activated users): "your results so far" against "what you lose on {{trial_end_date}}".**
   - Split: 50/50 of activated users who reach day 10.
   - Measure: share who add payment details by day 15. This is the test closest to revenue, so give it enough time and volume before you decide.

## 8. Metrics to track

**Primary metrics**

- Trial-to-paid conversion rate (the business outcome).
- Activation rate: share of trials that send a real invoice before the trial ends (the leading indicator this sequence is built around).

**Per-email metrics**

- Click-through rate, unsubscribe rate, spam complaint rate, and open rate (as a rough signal).
- For Track A emails: activation within 48 hours of the email.
- For emails 7 to 9: payment details added within 48 hours of the email.

**Sequence-level metrics**

- Time from signup to first invoice (median). Watch whether it moves earlier.
- Activated-to-paid rate, split by number of invoices sent in the trial (1, 2, 3 or more). If users with 2 or more invoices convert much better, make "second invoice" a formal goal of Track B.
- Drop-off points: at which Track A email do most eventual activators act? An email that drives almost nothing is a candidate to rewrite or remove.
- Extension take-up and the conversion rate of extended trials.
- Email 4 and email 9 reply reasons, tagged and counted every month.

**Review cadence**

Review weekly for the first month after launch, then monthly. Judge conversion changes by full trial cohorts (users who started in the same week), not by calendar week, because a trial takes at least 14 days to finish.

2. **Send product events to your email platform:** `trial_started`, `invoice_sent` (real, not test), `payment_details_added`, `trial_ended`, `trial_extended`. Include properties for invoices sent, amount invoiced, invoices paid and first client name.
