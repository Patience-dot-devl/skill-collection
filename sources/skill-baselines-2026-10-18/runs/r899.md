# Campaign Brief: Ferrous Postgres CDC Connector, 8-Week Lead-Gen Push

## Assumptions

You asked me not to stop for questions, so I built the plan on the assumptions below. Please correct any that are wrong, because several of them change the numbers.

- **Dates.** A prep week runs from 19 to 23 October 2026 (week 0), with no paid spend. The 8-week push runs from Monday 26 October to Friday 18 December 2026. The public launch is on Tuesday 3 November.
- **Product.** The connector is ready for production use, it ships in the open-source project, and Ferrous sells a paid tier (managed cloud or enterprise) that the sales team closes. I have not seen the connector's real differentiators. The messages in section 3 use common Postgres CDC pain points, and every product claim is marked **[confirm]** until engineering checks it.
- **Team.** About one and a half marketing people, one founder who sells, two account executives, and some engineering time (roughly 20% of two engineers) for workshops, technical content and architecture reviews.
- **Geography.** The campaign targets English-speaking buyers in Europe and North America. Live sessions run at 16:00 CET, which is 10:00 ET.
- **SQL definition.** A sales-qualified lead (SQL) is a person whom someone at Ferrous has spoken to, and who meets all of these conditions: they work at a company with 200 to 2,000 employees; they own or strongly influence data-platform decisions; they have a confirmed Postgres CDC use case; and they expect to act within 6 months. Sales must accept the lead in the CRM. Sales and marketing should sign off this definition in week 0.
- **Counting.** The target counts SQLs created between 26 October and 18 December. SQLs from campaign leads that qualify after that date are reported separately on 15 January.

## Read this first: the target is a stretch

300 SQLs from EUR 40,000 is a cash cost of about EUR 133 per SQL. Paid channels alone cannot reach that for a technical B2B buyer. In my working model, the EUR 22,000 of paid and sponsored media gives about 50 SQLs. The plan can only reach 300 if Ferrous's existing assets do most of the work: the 9k-star repository, the community around it, and outbound sales to accounts that already know the project. Those channels cost staff time, not cash.

There are two hard limits:

1. **Sales capacity.** 300 SQLs means about 430 qualification conversations in 8 weeks, which is 50 to 60 a week at the peak. Two account executives cannot do this alone. Section 4 explains how engineers, through the architecture-review offer, and the founder take part of the load.
2. **The calendar.** Week 5 includes US Thanksgiving (26 November), and week 8 runs into the December slowdown. The plan puts most of the effort into weeks 1 to 6.

My honest forecast is a likely range of **170 to 300 SQLs**. The plan is built to reach 300 if the target-case conversion rates hold. There is a formal checkpoint at the end of week 3 so you can see early which outcome is coming.

## 1. Campaign Overview

**Campaign name:** "Every Change, Captured". The alternative is "CDC Without the Pager".

**Summary:** An 8-week, engineer-led campaign that turns Ferrous's open-source reach into sales conversations about the new Postgres CDC connector, through live technical workshops, a free architecture-review offer, focused outbound, and targeted paid media.

**Primary objective:** Create 300 SQLs from data-platform leaders at companies with 200 to 2,000 employees between 26 October and 18 December 2026, at a cash cost of no more than EUR 135 per SQL.

**Secondary objectives:**
- Book 250 Postgres CDC architecture reviews, which are the main conversion event.
- Get 900 registrations across three live workshops, with 40% or more attending.
- Make the open-source repository a measurable pipeline source. Today it produces leads by chance; the aim is a tracked path from star or user to a sales conversation.
- Publish two customer proof assets (a case study or quoted results) that you can reuse after the campaign.

## 2. Target Audience

### Primary segment: data-platform leads (the buyers)

Titles include Head of Data Platform, Data Platform Engineering Manager, Data Infrastructure Lead, and Staff or Principal Data Engineer at companies with 200 to 2,000 employees. These companies run Postgres as a main operational database (self-managed, RDS or Aurora, Cloud SQL, AlloyDB, or similar) and move its data to a warehouse, a lakehouse, or a stream (Snowflake, BigQuery, Databricks, ClickHouse, Kafka or Iceberg).

> A data-platform lead at a growing software or digital company who struggles to keep Postgres data fresh in the warehouse without fragile Kafka Connect setups or per-row SaaS bills. They want reliable, low-latency replication that does not page their team. They usually find solutions through GitHub, peer recommendations, technical blogs and newsletters, and they care most about reliability, operational load, cost predictability and avoiding lock-in.

### Secondary segment: hands-on engineers (the champions)

These are data engineers and backend or platform engineers who star repositories, open issues and run proofs of concept. Many of them are already in your 9k-star audience. They rarely sign the contract, but they bring the buyer in. The campaign gives them material they can forward: benchmarks, a migration guide and a recording of a workshop.

### Tertiary segment: economic buyers at the smaller end

At companies with 200 to 500 employees, the VP of Engineering or the CTO often approves directly. They get a shorter, cost-and-risk version of the message in retargeting and outbound.

### Pain points and motivations

| Pain point | What it costs them | Motivation |
|---|---|---|
| Replication slots that keep WAL and fill the disk when a consumer stalls | Production incidents and on-call pages | "CDC must never threaten the primary database" |
| Running Debezium with Kafka and Kafka Connect only to move Postgres data | Engineering time spent on infrastructure that is not core to the business | Fewer moving parts |
| Initial snapshots and backfills of large tables | Days of work, load on the primary, inconsistent data | Backfill safely while streaming continues |
| Schema changes that break pipelines (logical decoding does not carry DDL) | Silent data gaps and broken dashboards | Pipelines that survive normal product development |
| Failover and major-version upgrades that lose slot position | Re-snapshots and data loss risk | Predictable behaviour during failover |
| Managed ELT tools priced by row volume | Bills that grow faster than the business | Predictable cost |

### Where they spend time

- **Code and community:** GitHub (your repository first), Hacker News, r/dataengineering, r/PostgreSQL, the dbt Community Slack, Data Talks Club, and Postgres community Slack and Discord spaces.
- **Newsletters and publications:** Postgres Weekly, Data Engineering Weekly, and similar engineer-written newsletters and Substacks.
- **Professional:** LinkedIn. Data-platform leads read here more than they post, and they respond to engineer-authored posts more than to company-page posts.
- **In person:** Postgres and data-engineering meetups and the conferences where you already give talks.

### Buying-stage alignment

| Stage | Who is there | Campaign job |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Engineers who do not yet have a CDC project | Launch post, workshop recordings, newsletter placements |
| Consideration | Teams that evaluate Debezium, managed ELT or a self-built solution | Field guide, comparison pages, workshops, benchmark |
| Decision | Teams with a project and a deadline | Architecture review, migration guide, proof of concept with an engineer |

The campaign spends most of its effort on consideration and decision, because the goal is SQLs within 8 weeks, not awareness.

## 4. Channel Strategy

Channels are listed in order of expected SQL contribution. The SQL figures are my target-case estimates. The low case is in the funnel table at the end of this section.

### 4.1 Open-source activation (owned): target 95 SQLs, EUR 0 in media

**Why it fits:** Your current pipeline already comes from the repository. Of 9,000 stargazers, I assume roughly 30 to 35% can be matched to a company, and roughly a fifth of those are in the 200 to 2,000 employee band. That gives about 600 people at about 400 ICP accounts who already know Ferrous, plus existing users who are likely to need Postgres CDC.

**Formats:**
- A README banner, release notes, a GitHub Discussions announcement, and a pinned issue for connector feedback.
- An "early access" email to your existing community and newsletter list in week 1, before the public launch.
- A "Talk to an engineer" link in the docs and in the connector's log output or CLI help, if your project culture accepts this [team decision].
- Account-level prioritisation. Use stargazer and contributor company data to rank accounts for outbound (section 4.2).

**Important constraint:** GitHub's Acceptable Use Policies restrict using information from GitHub, for example email addresses taken from profiles or commits, to send unsolicited email. Do not cold-email stargazers with addresses taken from GitHub. Use the data to choose accounts, then reach the data-platform lead through normal B2B prospecting, and talk to the stargazers themselves through your own channels (repository, community, opt-in lists). Have someone check the current policy wording and your GDPR position before week 1.

**Effort:** Medium. **Budget:** covered by the tooling line.

### 4.2 Signal-based outbound (owned, sales-led): target 55 SQLs

**Why it fits:** Buyers at 200 to 2,000 employee companies are few enough to name. Outbound that refers to a real signal works much better than generic sequences.

**Signals, in priority order:** (1) the company appears in your stargazer or contributor data; (2) someone from the company visited the docs or pricing pages (website visitor identification); (3) a job post mentions Debezium, Kafka Connect, CDC or "Postgres replication"; (4) the company attended a workshop or meetup.

**Formats:** Three sequences, one for each signal type. Each has three or four touches over 12 business days, using email and LinkedIn. The founder or an engineer signs the first email, and the offer is the architecture review. Target list: about 600 accounts and about 1,500 contacts.

**Effort:** High (sales time). **Budget:** enrichment and sequencing tools, inside the EUR 2,500 tooling line.

### 4.3 Live technical workshops (owned): target 45 SQLs, EUR 3,000

**Why it fits:** Technical buyers attend sessions where engineers show real systems. A workshop also gives you recordings that sales can use for months.

**Format:** Three 45-minute live sessions, each with a live demo and 15 minutes of questions. Every registrant gets the recording and an architecture-review offer.
- Thursday 12 November: "Postgres CDC in production: replication slots, WAL and failover"
- Thursday 3 December: "Backfilling a 1 TB table without downtime"
- Thursday 10 December: "Migrating off Debezium and Kafka Connect: a live walkthrough" (if possible with a destination partner, see 4.9)

**Effort:** High. **Budget:** EUR 1,500 production (platform, design, demo environment) and EUR 1,500 promotion.

### 4.4 LinkedIn ads (paid): target 20 SQLs, EUR 11,000

**Why it fits:** LinkedIn is the only paid channel where you can target by job title and company size together.

**Formats:**
- Thought-leader ads that promote posts by your engineers. These are usually more credible with engineers than company-page ads.
- Document ads with the field guide, using lead-generation forms.
- Workshop registration through lead-generation forms.
- Retargeting of docs and website visitors with the architecture-review offer.

**Targeting:** Job titles and functions for data engineering, data platform and data infrastructure; seniority from senior to director; company size 201 to 2,000; Europe and North America. Exclude existing customers.

**Effort:** Medium. **Budget:** EUR 11,000. Put 20% into testing formats in weeks 2 and 3, then move the money to the best-performing format. Refresh creative in week 5.

### 4.5 Google search ads (paid): target 15 SQLs, EUR 5,000

**Why it fits:** Search volume is small but intent is high. People who search for "postgres cdc", "debezium alternative", "postgres logical replication to snowflake" or "postgres to bigquery real time" usually have a project now.

**Formats:** Exact- and phrase-match campaigns pointing to the connector landing page and the comparison pages. Bid on competitor terms only where your comparison page is fair and accurate.

**Effort:** Medium. **Budget:** EUR 5,000.

### 4.6 Sponsored newsletters and communities (paid and earned): target 15 SQLs, EUR 6,000

**Why it fits:** Engineers trust curated newsletters more than ads.

**Formats:** Four or five placements in Postgres-focused and data-engineering newsletters between weeks 2 and 6, linking to the field guide or a workshop. Placement prices vary a lot. Get rate cards in week 0 and book early, because good slots often sell out weeks ahead.

**Effort:** Low. **Budget:** EUR 6,000. This line also works as the campaign's test budget: compare cost per SQL across placements and do not rebook the weak ones.

### 4.7 Launch content, SEO and organic community (owned and earned): target 30 SQLs

**Why it fits:** The launch is your one big moment for attention. Hacker News, Reddit and LinkedIn posts by your engineers can bring large spikes of the right audience.

**Formats:**
- An engineering launch post: "How we built Postgres CDC: slots, snapshots and DDL".
- A "Show HN" post on launch day, written by the engineer who built the connector.
- Posts on r/PostgreSQL and r/dataengineering that follow each community's self-promotion rules.
- Comparison pages and a migration guide that also target search.
- A self-reported attribution field ("How did you hear about us?") on every form. Developer-tool traffic is often word of mouth that UTMs miss.

**Effort:** Medium. **Budget:** production is inside the content line.

### 4.8 Meetups (owned and earned): target 15 SQLs, EUR 3,500

**Why it fits:** You already give conference talks, so an in-person evening suits the team. It also gives AEs high-quality conversations.

**Format:** Two evening meetups of 50 to 70 people each, in the two cities where GitHub location data shows the most stargazers. Amsterdam and London are likely candidates [check]. Hold one in week 4 and one in week 7. Run them with a local Postgres or data-engineering group where possible, with one Ferrous talk and one talk by a user or community member.

**Effort:** High. **Budget:** EUR 3,500 (venue, food, drinks, travel).

### 4.9 Partner co-marketing (earned): target 10 SQLs, nice-to-have

**Why it fits:** Destination vendors (warehouse, lakehouse, streaming) and Postgres hosting providers want customers to move data into their platforms.

**Format:** Co-host workshop 3, or get listed in a partner's integration directory and newsletter. This is opportunistic. Do not depend on it for the target.

**Effort:** Medium. **Budget:** EUR 0 planned. Use contingency if a strong opportunity appears.
